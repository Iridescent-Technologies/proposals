The risk

A model that improvises is a model you cannot trust.

A general-purpose LLM will happily generate fluent, confident content. It can be subtly wrong, out of date, or simply not how the job is actually done. In everyday chat that is an annoyance. In workplace learning, teaching the wrong thing is a compliance, safety and reputational risk. In anything regulated it is worse. You cannot put an ungrounded model in front of your workforce and hope.

The grounding

Three authoritative frameworks. Teach only where they meet.

Every Zavmo lesson sits in the overlap of three sources of truth. Outside that overlap there is nothing to teach. That is by design.

The three legs

What each framework guarantees.

JD

The job: what this role really requires

The learner's job description anchors learning to their actual role, not a generic template. It is the "for this person, in this job" filter that makes personalisation legitimate rather than arbitrary.

NOS

The standard: what competent practice looks like

National Occupational Standards define, at a national level, the skills and behaviours a competent person demonstrates in a role. This is the bar Zavmo teaches to. It is an external, published definition of "good", not the model's opinion.

OFQUAL

The qualification: regulated, assessed content

OFQUAL-regulated qualifications supply accredited learning content and assessment criteria. The curriculum is one a regulator already stands behind. So Zavmo delivers content that leads to a recognised credential, not improvised material.

How it holds

Retrieval, not improvisation.

The mechanism is deliberate. Zavmo does not ask the model "what should we teach about X?" and print the answer. It maps the learner's role to the relevant NOS skills, pulls the matching OFQUAL content from the standards graph, and has the model teach that. The material is retrieved and authoritative, not free-generated from the training data.

Anchored

Every lesson maps to a standard

If it cannot be traced to a framework, it is not taught. The overlap defines the scope. There is no "off-piste" region to wander into.

Checked

Quality-scored before it ships

Generated content is scored against the source, and the admin Prompt Studio lets teams test changes in simulation before a learner ever sees them.

Auditable

You can point to the source

Each lesson traces back to a JD, a NOS and a qualification. So "why were they taught this?" always has an answer your compliance team can stand behind.

Straight about what this is

This is not a claim that AI never errs. No honest vendor should make one. The claim is that every lesson is anchored to a standard you can point to. Grounding keeps teaching inside accepted best practice and makes it auditable. That is a very different promise from "trust the black box", and a far more defensible one.
