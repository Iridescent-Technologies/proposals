Section 1

What Future Fluency is, and what changes at Level 5

Future Fluency is about working well alongside AI and automation. Not building the technology. Using it, checking it, and leading a team through what it changes.

  • At Level 3 the learner changes their own daily work. They use AI tools on real tasks, check what comes back, automate something they repeat, and review what changed.
  • At Level 5 the learner leads a department through the same shift. Their own department is the evidence, not a case study about somebody else's.

Technical depth does not rise between the levels. Judgement, analysis and working with people do. A reader expecting deeper AI content at Level 5 will think the units have gone soft. They have not. The level rises through the complexity of the problem, not the difficulty of the tool.

How the units fit together. ZFF-GM-05 owns the judgement about the capability a department holds and will need. ZFF-EI-05 works with the people whose roles change. ZFF-AU-05 decides what could be automated and ZFF-DA-05 decides where effort should go. ZFF-PJ-05, the applied project, brings all four together in an annual plan. Where ZFF-AU-05 4.5 and ZFF-PJ-05 3.5 evaluate missing skills, they evaluate only the skills that piece of work needs, and leave the wider capability judgement to ZFF-GM-05.

The generic Diploma and its role versions

This page is the generic Diploma. It is written for a department of any kind. Each role version below is the same qualification: the same five units and the same 136 criteria, word for word, at the same size. A role version adds what that function brings to each unit: the change it faces, the rules it works within, and the evidence a learner would bring.

The share of guided learning falls as the level rises, and that is the descriptor doing its work. A Level 5 problem is only broadly defined. A large part of what the learner needs cannot be known in advance by anyone, including the tutor. The department, its systems and its people are the content, and they are found in the doing.

Two things are easy to misread in that table. Guided learning is not the same as time with Zavmo. It is the hours spent under direction. Tutoring is one part of that, not all of it. The other hours are not all project work. They are reflection, reading and personal investigation as well as the project, and most of it happens inside the learner's own job.

2.3 The level rule every criterion is written to

Every criterion in every unit is held against the Ofqual RQF Level 5 descriptor. Each unit's level check names the clause in short and lists the criteria that answer it.

Knowledge

Practical, theoretical or technological knowledge and understanding of a subject or field of work to find ways forward in broadly defined, complex contexts. Can analyse, interpret and evaluate relevant information, concepts and ideas. Is aware of the nature and scope of the area. Understands different perspectives, approaches or schools of thought and the reasoning behind them.

Skills

Determine, adapt and use appropriate methods, cognitive and practical skills to address broadly defined, complex problems. Use relevant research or development to inform actions. Evaluate actions, methods and results.

Against Level 4. Level 4 problems are complex and non-routine while normally fairly well defined. Level 5 problems are only broadly defined. The test applied throughout: has anybody told the learner what the problem is, what counts as a good answer, or which method to use? If any of the three is given, the criterion has dropped to Level 4. This is why determine appears so often. It is the descriptor's own word and it carries the level.

Against Level 6 and above. Absent from every criterion: critically analyse, design research, appraise, justify, and any outcome about evaluating implications. Also absent: reformulate and synthesise, which are Level 7.

Where compare is used. ZFF-GM-05 1.2, ZFF-EI-05 1.3, ZFF-AU-05 1.2 and ZFF-DA-05 1.4 open with compare, which on its own sits below Level 5. Each ends "and the reasoning behind each", which is the descriptor's clause on understanding different perspectives and the reasoning behind them. That ending carries the level. Shortening it would drop four criteria a level.

Scope is departmental. The change is happening to the learner's team or department and the learner leads a function through it. Level 3 was personal scope. The moment an outcome asks what the department is for, it has left Level 5 upward. The moment it takes only the learner's own tasks as its object, it has left Level 5 downward.

The subject is leading people through change. It is change management and working with people, applied to automation and data. It is not deep AI or dataset expertise. Technical depth peaks at Level 3 and does not rise up this ladder. Adding AI content to these units would mis-level them.

ZFF-GM-05

Leading a Department Through a Change in Required Capability

Level5TQT60 hoursCredits6GLH24 indicativeStatusMandatoryCriteria28

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to lead a department through a change in what its people are expected to be good at. The emphasis is on the judgement and the leading rather than on any particular skill being built. Learners establish what capability their department currently holds and what its work will require as its processes change, determine for themselves the criteria by which options for closing the difference will be evaluated, and evaluate those options using figures from their own systems and evidence of how comparable work has gone elsewhere. They then consult the people whose expected capability changes, adapt the recommendation in the light of what those conversations establish, set out what each person is expected to do and what support is available, and evaluate what the change produced in the department's work.

Scope is departmental. Keeping the learner's own skills current is ZFF-GM-03 and is outside this unit.

Design note. This unit assesses what the learner does, not the attitude anyone reports holding. Every criterion is an observable action, a stated determination, or a judgement made against evidence that can be produced. No criterion asks the learner to demonstrate a growth mindset, and no criterion asks an assessor to judge a learner's or a colleague's attitude, resilience or confidence. Those would not be assessable.

Level check

Broadly defined, complex contexts: LO2 and LO3. Nobody states which capabilities matter, what the department should be able to do next, or how the difference should be closed. 2.1 and 3.1 require the learner to determine the method and the criteria before any option can be evaluated.

Analyse, interpret and evaluate: 1.1, 2.5 and 4.3 analyse; 3.3 interprets; 1.3, 1.5, 2.6, 3.5, 3.6, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3 and 5.4 evaluate.

Nature and scope of the area: 1.4, 2.2, 2.3. The learner establishes what the department's roles require and what its people can already do before proposing anything.

Different perspectives and their reasoning: 1.2 and 1.5. Both require the reasoning, not only the position.

Determine, adapt and use methods: 2.1, 2.4, 3.1 and 4.6 determine; 4.4 adapts; LO3 and LO4 use.

Research informs action: 3.4, which uses investigation of comparable work to inform the choice rather than to describe the field.

Evaluate actions, methods and results: LO5 in full, and 4.3. 5.4 evaluates the method and the criteria the learner determined rather than ones they were given.

Boundary against Level 3. ZFF-GM-03 is the closest unit in the suite and the difference has to be visible in the wording. There the learner keeps their own skills current: they compare their own skills with what their own role will require, plan one piece of their own learning, and review what it changed. Here the learner establishes what a department can already do, determines what it will need, evaluates the options for closing the difference, and leads the people through the change. No criterion in this unit takes the learner's own skills as its object. Determine, analyse and interpret appear here and appear nowhere in ZFF-GM-03.

Boundary against Level 4. A Level 4 version would read: assess your team against the competency framework you have been given, and choose from the training options on the approved list, within the budget you have been set. Every one of those is a definition supplied from outside. Removing all three is what makes this Level 5. 3.1 carries the level. If the criteria for evaluating options are supplied as a competency framework or a template rather than determined by the learner, the unit becomes Level 4 and the qualification's level claim goes with it. The same applies to 2.1: supplying a skills-audit instrument drops the unit a level.

Boundary against Level 6 and above. No criterion asks the learner to critically analyse, to design research, or to evaluate implications beyond the department. 3.4 uses investigation that already exists. 5.2 evaluates the effect on the department's own work, which is scope-bounded. Two moves are above the level and are excluded: reconsidering what the department's roles are for, and changing what the organisation as a whole expects of its people. Where the recommendation genuinely requires an organisational change to proceed, the correct treatment is to record it at 1.4 as a constraint, not to widen the unit.

Assessment guidance

  • LO5 and elapsed time. Where the change has had too little time to take full effect within the unit, the learner evaluates the effect seen so far and states its limits: the period it covers and what it cannot yet show. That meets 5.1 to 5.3. See section 4.9.
  • Consultation records. 4.2 records what colleagues raised. Colleagues are identified by role in the portfolio, not by name. See section 4.8.

Evidence

Zavmo records the learner's work on this unit inside the platform: which outcomes and criteria they opened, what the tutor put to them, what they answered, where their reasoning was working at the analyse level the unit asks for, and the moment each outcome was recorded as met. It records nothing about the department itself. Every statement the builder produces names the outcome and the criteria it bears on, so a statement made while the learner works on LO3 carries learning_outcome_id of LO3 and assessment_criteria_addressed of 3.1 and 3.6. The assessor reads the record criterion by criterion rather than as a session log.

What the platform record does not reach

Most of LO4, and the parts of LO2, LO3 and LO5 that reach into the department, happen where there is no telemetry.

  • 4.4 adapts the recommendation. The changed recommendation is a document. What makes it assessable is the learner's account of what changed and why, tested in discussion.
  • LO5 evaluates effects in the department's own work. No platform event reaches them.

ZFF-EI-05

Working with the People Whose Roles Change

Level5TQT60 hoursCredits6GLH24 indicativeStatusMandatoryCriteria27

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to work with the people whose roles change when a department automates part of its work, including the people who do not want the change. The emphasis is on what the department establishes and what it then does, rather than on any single conversation. Learners establish who is affected and what changes for each of them, determine for themselves the method by which they will find out what those people raise, and analyse what is raised to separate objections to the change itself from objections to the way it is being made. They then determine the criteria by which the department's response will be evaluated, decide which points change the department's plans and which do not, work directly with the people concerned including those who continue not to accept the change, and evaluate what the whole of it produced in the department's work.

Scope is departmental. Strengthening one working relationship is not this unit. What the organisation as a whole should do about role change is set elsewhere and is outside it.

Design note. This unit assesses what the learner does, not the emotional state anyone reports holding. Every criterion is an observable action, a stated determination, or a judgement made against recorded evidence. No criterion asks the learner to demonstrate empathy, and no criterion asks an assessor to rate a learner's self-awareness or regulation. Those would not be assessable. Emotional intelligence is what the unit builds. The conduct of a real change, and its record, are what it assesses.

Level check

Broadly defined, complex contexts: LO2 and LO3. Nobody tells the learner how to find out what people think, what counts as a point worth acting on, or where the line falls between changing the plan and holding it. 2.2, 3.1 and 3.3 require the learner to determine all three.

Analyse, interpret and evaluate: 1.1, 2.4 and 3.2 analyse; 2.5 interprets; 1.2, 1.4, 2.6, 3.2, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3 and 5.4 evaluate. 2.3 to 2.5 run the descriptor's verbs in its own order across a single body of recorded evidence.

Nature and scope of the area: 1.5, 2.1, 2.3. The learner establishes who is affected, who depends on them, and what the department is obliged to do, before deciding anything.

Different perspectives and their reasoning: 1.2 and 1.3, and 2.4. 2.4 makes the learner separate the objection to the change from the objection to how it is being made, which are different positions with different reasoning.

Determine, adapt and use methods: 2.2, 3.1, 3.3, 3.6, 4.4 and 4.5 determine; 3.5 adapts; LO2 and LO4 use.

Research informs action: 3.4, which uses investigation of comparable changes to inform the response rather than to describe the field.

Evaluate actions, methods and results: LO5 in full, and 3.2. 5.4 evaluates the method and the criteria the learner determined rather than ones they were given.

Boundary against Level 3. ZFF-EI-03 is the closest unit in the suite. There the learner names and manages their own emotions and reads the people they deal with directly. Here the object is every person whose role changes, the people whose work depends on those roles, and the department's own output. The learner determines the method, sets the criteria, decides which objections move the plan, and answers for the result. No criterion in this unit takes the learner's own emotions as its object.

Boundary against Level 4. A Level 4 version would supply the list of people to consult, a briefing script to work from, and a decision already taken about which objections may be conceded. 2.2, 3.1 and 3.3 exist specifically to remove all three. 3.1 and 3.3 carry the level. A change-management template that fixes what counts as an acceptable response defines the problem for the learner and drops the unit a level.

Boundary against Level 6 and above. No criterion asks the learner to critically analyse, to design research, or to evaluate implications beyond the department. 3.4 uses investigation that already exists. 5.2 evaluates the effect on the department's own work. Two moves are above the level and are excluded: reconsidering what the department's roles are for, and changing how the organisation as a whole handles role change. Where the response genuinely requires either, the correct treatment is to record it at 1.5 as a constraint the department is working within, not to widen the unit.

Assessment guidance

  • 4.3 needs a stated position. The person must have said that they do not accept the change, in the consultation at 2.3 or since. The learner's own inference that someone disagrees does not meet 4.3.
  • 3.2 and 4.3 where nobody objects. If no one in the department has stated that they do not accept the change, a person who raised a point the department did not accept meets both. If there is no such person either, a simulated discussion may be used for 4.3, with the reason recorded. The other person is played by a human in role, the assessor or the line manager, using a position recorded at 2.3 or found at 3.4. A role-play with the AI tutor does not meet 4.3. See section 4.7.
  • 3.6 is a determination. A recorded determination meets 3.6. No learner is required to evidence a disciplinary or contractual action against a colleague in order to pass this unit.
  • LO5 and elapsed time. Where the change has had too little time to take full effect, the learner evaluates the effect seen so far and states its limits. See section 4.9.
  • Consultation records. 2.3, 4.1 and 4.3 record what colleagues raised. Colleagues are identified by role in the portfolio, not by name. See section 4.8.

What Zavmo records is the preparation and the practice: the dialogue that builds LO1, the role-play or case study the tutor put in front of the learner, the questions the learner asked rather than answered. What it cannot record is the conversation at 4.3 with a colleague who does not accept the change, which is the unit's hardest criterion and its most important one.

ZFF-AU-05

Planning Automation Across a Department

Level5TQT80 hoursCredits8GLH46 indicativeStatusMandatoryCriteria26

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to work out what in a department's work could be automated, what should not be, and what it would cost to find out. The emphasis is on the judgement rather than the tooling. Learners establish what their department actually does and where its effort goes, determine for themselves the criteria a candidate process has to meet, analyse individual processes against those criteria using figures from their own systems, and test that evaluation against how comparable work has gone elsewhere. They then consult the people whose work would change, adapt the evaluation in the light of what those conversations establish, and put a recommendation to whoever would decide.

Scope is departmental. The unit stops at a recommendation on a set of candidate processes. Turning that into a sequenced, resourced annual plan is ZFF-PJ-05.

1.4Identify the constraints that limit what own department can automate, including systems, contracts, permissions and skills
1.5Evaluate the different views held within own department about automation, and the reasoning given for them

Level5TQT70 hoursCredits7GLH40 indicativeStatusMandatoryCriteria25

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to use the data their department already produces to decide where its effort should go. The emphasis is on the direction rather than the arithmetic. Learners establish what their department's measures actually count, what behaviour each one encourages in the people it reports on, and what they leave out. ZFF-PJ-05

Applied Project: Annual Automation Plan for Own Department

Level5TQT140 hoursCredits14GLH10 indicativeStatusMandatoryCriteria30

Unit aim

This unit is the applied project that carries the Diploma to its size, and it is the qualification's anchor. Over a period long enough for the department's own year to be visible in the figures, the learner maps what their department actually does, determines which processes are candidates for automation and which are not, costs the options with real numbers from real systems, consults the people whose work would change, sequences the work across a year, builds in the checks and the fallbacks, and presents the plan to whoever signs departmental plans off.

The deliverable is stated in the brief. Almost nothing else is: not which processes, not what counts as a benefit, not what a good sequence looks like, not whose agreement is needed, and not whether the answer is to automate much or little.

Delivery note. The 140 hours are almost entirely the learner's own work, carried out in their job. A departmental planning project run across a year generates continuous, time-stamped, real-work evidence. That is normally the hardest thing to produce for a qualification at this level, and the most expensive for a conventional provider to assess.

Level check

This is the unit most likely to be challenged on level, in both directions. The challenge from below is that a stated deliverable makes the problem well defined. The challenge from above is that an annual plan for a department sounds strategic. Both are answered here rather than left to a review.

Broadly defined, complex contexts: LO1, LO2. See the paragraph below on what is and is not given.

Analyse, interpret and evaluate: 2.2, 3.3 analyse; 2.3 interpret; 1.5, 2.5, 2.6, 3.5, 4.6 evaluate.

What is given, and what is not. The brief names the deliverable: an annual automation plan for your department. That is the whole of what is stated. It does not name the processes, the criteria, the method, the measure of benefit, the people who must agree, the shape of the year, or the answer. Two competent learners in the same department would produce different plans and both could be sound. That is the descriptor's broadly defined, and it is why the same brief at Level 3 is not the same kind of brief at all. There the object was named and only the instance was chosen.

What would push it to Level 4. Supplying the criteria at 2.1, supplying the process list at 1.2, or supplying a business case template that fixes what counts as a benefit. The assessment guidance below says so, because supplying a template is exactly the kind of helpfulness a delivery team adds later without realising what it costs.

What would push it to Level 7. Both exclusions are explicit. First, reframing what the department is for. Second, producing a plan that changes how the organisation as a whole operates. A learner who produces either has produced work above the level, and the criterion is still not met. Assessors find this counter-intuitive and need telling. Where a plan genuinely requires an organisational change to proceed, the correct treatment is to record it at 4.2 as a dependency outside the department, not to widen the plan.

Not Level 6. Nothing asks the learner to critically analyse, to design research, or to evaluate implications. 4.2 identifies dependencies. It does not place the learner in a context where many factors interact and none can be held constant.

Section 4

Assessment and quality assurance

The same model applies to every unit.

4.1 Portfolio of evidence from real work

Assessment is by portfolio drawn from work the learner is already responsible for, gathered over the period of the unit. There is no examination.

4.2 The AI teaches. A human assessor decides

Zavmo's AI tutors deliver the learning and prompt the learner to produce and submit evidence. The AI does not make the assessment decision. Every decision on every criterion is made by a named, occupationally competent, appropriately qualified human assessor, internally verified by a qualified IQA against a sampling plan, and sampled by the awarding organisation's External Quality Assurer.

4.3 How evidence is captured

Learner activity in the platform is captured as xAPI statements and assembled into the portfolio: the tasks they selected, the instruction sets they wrote and rewrote, the outputs the tool returned, the checks they ran, the amendments they made and the reasons they gave. This gives the assessor a time-stamped record of the learner's process rather than only a finished artefact. The xAPI record is evidence presented to the assessor. It is not an assessment decision. Section 5 sets out which events fire today, which are only wired, and what the record does not reach.

4.4 Professional discussion is mandatory

At least one recorded professional discussion per unit, conducted by the qualified assessor. This is the primary control for authenticating the learner at distance and confirming the portfolio is their own work.

4.5 Declared AI use

Learners are expected to use AI in producing evidence, because that is the subject of the qualification. All AI use in evidence must be declared. The assessment decision rests on the learner's judgement about the AI output, not on the polish of the output itself, and that judgement is confirmed in live discussion.

4.6 Suggested evidence

Records of professional discussion and oral questioning for the knowledge outcomes. Product evidence, annotated by the learner, for the skills outcomes. Witness testimony from a line manager where a criterion turns on something that happened in real work. Reflective accounts for the review outcomes. These are suggestions and centres could use other evidence, provided the criteria are covered and the evidence can be internally and externally verified.

4.7 Real-work grounding, and when real work does not supply the moment

The tasks used must be tasks the learner is already responsible for. A simulated task is used only where the learner's real work does not supply what a criterion needs, and the reason is recorded. Two cases are expected: the role offers no suitable task, or nobody in the department disagrees with the change (ZFF-EI-05 3.2 and 4.3, whose guidance sets out what meets them). A third case is not simulation. Where no decision maker is available within the period of the unit (ZFF-AU-05 5.3, ZFF-DA-05 4.5), a presentation to a person senior to the learner, with authority over the department's work, who will take it to the decision maker, meets those criteria, with the reason recorded. ZFF-PJ-05 5.1 and 5.2 have no fallback: the plan is presented to the person who signs it off.

4.8 Colleagues in the portfolio

Several criteria record what colleagues raised about a change to their own roles: ZFF-GM-05 4.2, ZFF-EI-05 2.3, 4.1 and 4.3, ZFF-AU-05 4.2, ZFF-DA-05 4.3 and ZFF-PJ-05 3.2. In submitted evidence colleagues are identified by role, not by name. Where a role identifies one person, a label such as Colleague A is used instead. Each colleague is told that what they raise may be recorded, without their name, as evidence for a qualification. Where the assessor needs to confirm who said what, that is done in the professional discussion. The recording of that discussion is portfolio evidence and is kept under the same rule. Portfolio evidence is kept only for the period the awarding organisation requires.

4.9 Effects that need time to show

ZFF-GM-05, ZFF-EI-05 and ZFF-DA-05 each end by evaluating the effect of a change. A change made inside a unit may not have shown its full effect by the time the unit ends. The learner then evaluates the effect seen so far and states its limits: the period the evidence covers and what it cannot yet show. That meets the criterion only if at least one measurement has been taken after the change, against the measure set earlier in the unit. A forecast of the effect does not meet it. The criteria are not reduced, because evaluating what a change produced is the descriptor clause those outcomes answer. ZFF-PJ-05 runs across the department's own year and plans the measure at 4.7 rather than evaluating an effect.

Section 5

The evidence trail

Zavmo writes every learner action as an xAPI statement. For a qualification assessed from real work carried out over months, that record is the difference between an assessor reading a finished artefact and an assessor reading how it was made. This section sets out what the record holds, how a statement reaches an individual assessment criterion, and, at equal length, what it does not hold.

One store, written from the back end

Statements use the xAPI vocabulary of actor, verb, object, context and result. There is no external learning record store. Every statement is a row in a single Postgres table inside Zavmo's own service, and nothing is mirrored anywhere else. Statements are written by the back end and never by the learner's browser, because a statement posted by a learner is a statement a learner controls. Each row carries two times: when the thing happened, and when it was stored.

How a statement reaches an assessment criterion

This is the part that matters for a regulated qualification, and it is a property of the code rather than an intention. The statement builder's build_statement takes learning_outcome_id and assessment_criteria as required arguments, alongside learning_objective_id. A statement that cannot name the outcome and the criteria it bears on does not get built. Ten extensions are mandatory on every statement. Three words, used strictly

Every event named on this page carries one of three statuses. They mean different things and are never used interchangeably.

Observed

Seen firing in the production store. This is the only status that means a record of this kind already exists.

Wired

A live emit call site exists in the code, confirmed by a static analysis that the build re-runs and fails on if it drifts. The event has not been seen firing.

Catalogued

Defined in the event registry with no emit call site. Named here so a reviewer sees the whole shape. Not evidence of anything.

What xAPI never evidences, at any status

At Level 5 a large share of the work happens in meetings, in consultation with people, and in documents produced in the learner's own workplace. No amount of wiring reaches any of it.

  • Consultation. ZFF-GM-05 LO4, ZFF-EI-05 LO4, ZFF-AU-05 LO4, ZFF-DA-05 4.3 and ZFF-PJ-05 LO3 all turn on conversations with colleagues about changes to their own work. The evidence is the consultation record the learner makes, witness testimony from a line manager, and the recorded professional discussion.
  • Presentation to a decision maker. ZFF-AU-05 5.3, ZFF-DA-05 4.5 and ZFF-PJ-05 5.1 to 5.3 require the learner to put a recommendation to the person who would decide and to answer challenge on it. Nothing in the platform sees that room.
  • The department's own systems. Every criterion that reads "using figures from the department's own systems" draws on systems Zavmo does not hold and will not hold.
  • Work in the learner's own tools, including AI used outside Zavmo. ZFF-DA-05 3.6 is the clearest case and is dealt with in that unit's section.

What does not move

Section 4 states it and this section does not change it. The AI teaches. A human assessor decides. The xAPI record is evidence presented to a named, occupationally competent assessor, and it is never an assessment decision. Where a statement records that a validating agent checked a submission, what is recorded is that a check ran and what it said. The decision on the criterion is still the assessor's, is internally verified, and is sampled by the awarding organisation.

Where these statuses come from

Every status on this page is derived from Zavmo's own engineering records rather than stated from memory: the emission audit, regenerated by a static analysis of the code, which fails the build if the recorded status drifts from what the code does; the production observation snapshot, taken read-only against the production store on 17 July 2026; and the vendored event registry, which is hash-guarded against its upstream copy. Any of the three can be re-run.

Section 6

What we know is not yet right

Listed plainly, so that a reviewer has this list before finding any of it.

1

No assessor has read these units. They are written to the descriptor and internally checked. Nobody with assessment experience has read them. This is the largest single gap.

2

TQT is allocated, not measured. The unit hours are judgements that produce a defensible total. If any unit's hours are revised down, the Diploma needs re-checking against the 370 hour threshold. It has 40 hours of headroom.

3

Whether AI-led tutoring counts as guided learning is unresolved. GLH is shown as indicative throughout. The split in section 2.2 depends on the answer.

4

How knowledge criteria may be evidenced is unresolved. We have assumed recorded tutoring dialogue can evidence knowledge outcomes rather than written tests. That assumption favours our model, so it should not be taken on our word. If written assessment is required, the hours split needs revisiting.

5

The assessor layer does not exist yet. We do not yet employ occupationally competent assessors or a qualified IQA. Section 4 describes what we will put in place, not what is running today.

6

The register check is not repeated on this page. Section 7 of the Gateway specification pack compares both Diplomas with the Ofqual register extract of 2 September 2026. The units have changed since, so it should be re-run before submission.

7

The evidence trail has not run for this Diploma. No learner has been registered against it, so none of the events on this page has fired for it. The events that carry a criterion decision, ZAV-CRT-002 to ZAV-CRT-004 and the ZAV-OFQ family, are wired and have never been seen in production. At the July measurement, 19 per cent of stored statements carried a named event id.

8

Demand figures are excluded on purpose. We hold internal estimates of which fluencies are most in demand. They are not evidence and they are not in this specification.
