Section 1

What Future Fluency is, and what changes at Level 5.

Future Fluency is about working well alongside AI and automation. Not building the technology. Using it, checking it, and leading a team through what it changes.

The subject moves as the level rises, and it moves deliberately. At Level 3 the learner changes their own daily work. They use AI tools on real tasks, check what comes back, automate something they repeat, and review what changed. At Level 5 the learner leads a department through the same shift. Technical depth does not rise. Judgement, analysis and working with people does.

This matters for reviewing the specification. A reader expecting deeper AI content at Level 5 will think the units have gone soft. They have not. The level rises through the complexity of the problem, not the difficulty of the tool.

The share of guided learning falls as the level rises, and that is the descriptor doing its work. A Level 5 problem is only broadly defined. A large part of what the learner needs cannot be known in advance by anyone, including the tutor. The department, its systems and its people are the content, and they are found in the doing.

Section 3

The level rule every criterion is written to.

Every criterion in every unit below is held against the Ofqual RQF Level 5 descriptor.

Knowledge

Practical, theoretical or technological knowledge and understanding of a subject or field of work to find ways forward in broadly defined, complex contexts. Can analyse, interpret and evaluate relevant information, concepts and ideas. Is aware of the nature and scope of the area. Understands different perspectives, approaches or schools of thought and the reasoning behind them.

Skills

Determine, adapt and use appropriate methods, cognitive and practical skills to address broadly defined, complex problems. Use relevant research or development to inform actions. Evaluate actions, methods and results.

Against Level 4

Level 4 problems are complex and non-routine while normally fairly well defined. Level 5 problems are only broadly defined. The test applied throughout: has anybody told the learner what the problem is, what counts as a good answer, or which method to use? If any of the three is given, the criterion has dropped to Level 4. This is why determine appears so often. It is the descriptor's own word and it is load-bearing.

Against Level 6 and above

Deliberately absent from every unit: critically analyse, design research, appraise, and any outcome about evaluating implications. Also absent: reformulate and synthesise, which are Level 7. Justify does not appear either, because it appears nowhere in the three units this specification is levelled against.

One exception is visible and is flagged rather than hidden. ZFF-AU-05 uses appraise in its LO3 stem and at 3.1, 3.4, 4.3, 4.4 and 5.5, in the ordinary sense of weighing candidate processes against criteria the learner has set. It is not carrying the Level 6 descriptor sense. The wording is reproduced here as it stands so that a reviewer sees it, and it is one of the things we would like a view on.

Scope is departmental

The change is happening to the learner's team or department and the learner leads a function through it. Level 3 was personal scope. The moment an outcome asks what the department is for, it has left Level 5 upward. The moment it takes only the learner's own tasks as its object, it has left Level 5 downward.

The subject is leading people through change

It is change management and working with people, applied to automation and data. It is explicitly not deep AI or dataset expertise. Technical depth peaks at Level 3 and does not rise up this ladder. Someone will eventually try to improve these units by adding AI content. That would mis-level them.

Section 4

Two units have been rewritten.

ZFF-GM-05 and ZFF-EI-05 were written earlier than the other three and to a different house style. They were not fit to submit. They have been rewritten in full and the replacements appear below, marked Rewritten wherever they occur.

What was wrong with the previous versions

1. Personal scope in a departmental qualification

Every criterion took the learner's own mindset, own regulation or own relationships as its object. The level rule the other three units are written to says plainly that a Level 5 outcome taking only the learner's own tasks as its object has left Level 5 downward. As written, the two sat closer to Level 3, with no visible difference in scope from ZFF-GM-03.

2. The wrong verb band

Appraise is treated as Level 6 throughout the other units. Formulate is Level 7. Justify appeared in both and appears nowhere in the three departmental units. All three have been removed.

3. Far too thin

Eight assessment criteria each, against 25 to 30 in the other three, and against 60 hours of Total Qualification Time each. That is not a small difference in a Diploma. It changes what an assessor has to evidence and it looks inconsistent to an External Quality Assurer reading the qualification whole.

4. Some criteria were not assessable

"Assess own mindset across defined domains" names no standard against which the assessment is made, and mindset is not directly observable. ZFF-GM-03 solves the same problem at Level 3 by assessing what the learner does rather than the attitude they report holding. That is the precedent the rewrites follow.

Unit 1 · ZFF-GM-05

Leading a Department Through a Change in Required Capability

Rewritten
Level 5TQT 60Credits 6 GLH 24Status Mandatory 5 outcomes · 28 criteria

Newly rewritten · what changed and why

This unit replaces the previous ZFF-GM-05, titled Growth Mindset, which had four outcomes and eight criteria, all of them taking the learner's own mindset as their object.

  • Scope moved from personal to departmental. The object of every criterion is now the capability a department holds, the capability it will require, and the people whose expected capability changes.
  • The verb band was corrected. Appraise, formulate and justify are gone. The unit now uses the descriptor's own verbs: determine, analyse, interpret, evaluate, adapt and use.
  • Granularity was brought into line. Eight criteria became 28, matching the 25 to 30 carried by the other three units for comparable hours.
  • Every criterion is now observable. Following the ZFF-GM-03 precedent, nothing asks the learner to demonstrate that they hold a growth mindset, and nothing asks an assessor to judge an attitude. The mindset is what the unit builds. The actions are what it assesses.

TQT, credits and guided learning hours are unchanged, so the Diploma remains at 410 TQT and 41 credits.

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to lead a department through a change in what its people are expected to be good at. The emphasis is on the judgement and the leading rather than on any particular skill being built. Learners establish what capability their department currently holds and what its work will require as its processes change, determine for themselves the criteria by which options for closing the difference will be evaluated, and evaluate those options using figures from their own systems and evidence of how comparable work has gone elsewhere. They then consult the people whose expected capability changes, adapt the recommendation in the light of what those conversations establish, set out what each person is expected to do and what support is available, and evaluate what the change produced in the department's work.

Scope is departmental. Keeping the learner's own skills current is ZFF-GM-03 and is outside this unit.

A design note that is load-bearing: this unit assesses what the learner does, not the attitude anyone reports holding. Every criterion is an observable action, a stated determination, or a judgement made against evidence that can be produced. No criterion asks the learner to demonstrate a growth mindset, and no criterion asks an assessor to judge a learner's or a colleague's attitude, resilience or confidence. Those would not be assessable.

Boundary against Level 3

ZFF-GM-03 is the closest unit in the suite and the difference has to be visible in the wording. There the learner keeps their own skills current: they compare their own skills with what their own role will require, plan one piece of their own learning, and review what it changed. Here the learner establishes what a department can already do, determines what it will need, evaluates the options for closing the difference, and leads the people through the change. No criterion in this unit takes the learner's own skills as its object. Determine, analyse and interpret appear here and appear nowhere in ZFF-GM-03.

Boundary against Level 4

A Level 4 version would read: assess your team against the competency framework you have been given, and choose from the training options on the approved list, within the budget you have been set. Every one of those is a definition supplied from outside. Removing all three is what makes this Level 5. Watch 3.1 if the unit is ever edited. Two moves are above the level and are excluded: reconsidering what the department's roles are for, and changing what the organisation as a whole expects of its people. Where the recommendation genuinely requires an organisational change to proceed, the correct treatment is to record it at 1.4 as a constraint, not to widen the unit.

Unit 2 · ZFF-EI-05

Working with the People Whose Roles Change

Rewritten
Level 5TQT 60Credits 6 GLH 24Status Mandatory 5 outcomes · 27 criteria

Newly rewritten · what changed and why

This unit replaces the previous ZFF-EI-05, titled Emotional Intelligence, which had four outcomes and eight criteria, all of them taking the learner's own self-awareness, own regulation or a single working relationship as their object.

  • Scope moved from personal to departmental. One relationship is not a department. The object of every criterion is now the whole group of people whose roles change, the people whose work depends on those roles, and the department's own work.
  • The verb band was corrected. Justify is gone, along with the assess-own-state constructions. The unit now uses the descriptor's own verbs: determine, analyse, interpret, evaluate, adapt and use.
  • Granularity was brought into line. Eight criteria became 27, matching the 25 to 30 carried by the other three units for comparable hours.
  • Every criterion is now observable. Nothing asks a learner to report their own emotional state and nothing asks an assessor to rate empathy. What is assessed is what the learner established, what they decided, what they said to whom, and what followed.
  • The people who do not want the change are now in the unit. The previous version had no criterion covering disagreement that is not resolved. 2.6, 3.2, 3.6 and 4.3 cover it directly.

TQT, credits and guided learning hours are unchanged, so the Diploma remains at 410 TQT and 41 credits.

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to work with the people whose roles change when a department automates part of its work, including the people who do not want the change. The emphasis is on what the department establishes and what it then does, rather than on any single conversation. Learners establish who is affected and what changes for each of them, determine for themselves the method by which they will find out what those people raise, and analyse what is raised to separate objections to the change itself from objections to the way it is being made. They then determine the criteria by which the department's response will be evaluated, decide which points change the department's plans and which do not, work directly with the people concerned including those who continue not to accept the change, and evaluate what the whole of it produced in the department's work.

Scope is departmental. Strengthening one working relationship is not this unit. What the organisation as a whole should do about role change is set elsewhere and is outside it.

A design note that is load-bearing: this unit assesses what the learner does, not the emotional state anyone reports holding. Every criterion is an observable action, a stated determination, or a judgement made against recorded evidence. No criterion asks the learner to demonstrate empathy, and no criterion asks an assessor to rate a learner's self-awareness or regulation. Those would not be assessable. Emotional intelligence is what the unit builds. The conduct of a real change, and its record, are what it assesses.

Unit aim

Unit 4 · ZFF-DA-05

Using Data to Direct a Department's Priorities

Unchanged
Level 5TQT 70Credits 7 GLH 40Status Mandatory 5 outcomes · 25 criteria

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to use the data their department already produces to decide where its effort should go. The emphasis is on the direction rather than the arithmetic. Learners establish what their department's measures actually count, what behaviour each one encourages in the people it reports on, and what they leave out. They then determine a question about the department's priorities that data could help settle, determine the method for answering it, analyse what the data indicates, and test that reading against an alternative one and against how comparable work behaves elsewhere.

Scope is departmental. The learner directs the priorities of a function, which includes changing what that function measures. What the department exists to achieve is set elsewhere and is outside this unit. The unit assumes no specialist statistical training and none is required to meet the criteria.

Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

LO1

The learner will: Know what a department's measures show and what they drive.

RefThe learner can
1.1Analyse what the measures reported by own department actually count, and over what period
1.2Analyse the behaviour each measure encourages in the people whose work it reports on
1.3Evaluate what own department's current measures leave out
1.4Compare different approaches to measuring work of this kind, and the reasoning behind each
1.5Evaluate the different views held within own department about what its measures show, and the reasoning given for them
LO2

The learner will: Be able to determine a question about a department's priorities that data could help settle.

RefThe learner can
2.1State the question about own department's priorities that the work will address, and the reasons for selecting it
2.2Determine the data that bears on the question, and state the reasons for the selection
2.3Identify data that was available but set aside, and evaluate the effect of setting it aside
2.4Determine the method to be used to work with the data, and state the reasons for choosing it
2.5Evaluate the quality of the data obtained, and state how gaps and errors were handled
LO3

The learner will: Be able to analyse a department's data and interpret what it indicates.

RefThe learner can
3.1Analyse the selected data in relation to the question
3.2Interpret what the analysis indicates about where the department's effort is going
3.3Identify an interpretation the same data would also support, and evaluate which of the two it better supports
3.4Use relevant investigation, inside or outside own organisation, to test whether the interpretation holds
3.5Evaluate the limits of what the data can show, and what would be needed to settle the question
3.6Identify where an AI or automated tool was used in the analysis, and evaluate what was checked and why
LO4

The learner will: Be able to direct a department's priorities from what data indicates.

RefThe learner can
4.1Determine the changes to the department's priorities that follow from the interpretation
4.2Evaluate what the department would stop or reduce in order to make the changes possible
4.3Consult the people whose work the changes would affect, and record what was raised
4.4Adapt the proposed changes in response to what the consultation established, and state what changed
4.5Present the proposed priorities, with the evidence, to the person who decides them, and respond to challenge
4.6State how the effect of the changes will be measured, and over what period
LO5

The learner will: Be able to evaluate the effect of directing priorities from data.

RefThe learner can
5.1Evaluate the effect of the changed priorities against what was stated at 4.6, using evidence
5.2Evaluate how effective the method determined at 2.4 was, giving reasons
5.3Identify what would be determined differently next time, and the reasons

Boundary against Level 3

ZFF-DA-03 is the closest unit in the suite and the difference has to be visible in the wording. In ZFF-DA-03 the learner states a question about their own work and recommends to the person who decides. Here the learner determines a question about a department's priorities, determines the method, changes what the department measures, and consults the people affected. Analyse and determine appear here and appear nowhere in ZFF-DA-03.

Boundary against Level 4

A Level 4 version would supply the question and the reporting pack. 2.1 and 2.4 exist specifically to remove both.

Boundary against Level 6

Nothing asks the learner to critically analyse or to design research, to evaluate implications beyond the department, or to reconsider what the department is measuring for, which is the Level 7 move.

Unit 5 · ZFF-PJ-05

Applied Project: Annual Automation Plan for Own Department

Unchanged
Level 5TQT 140Credits 14 GLH 10Status Mandatory 5 outcomes · 30 criteria

Unit aim

This unit is the applied project that carries the Diploma to its size, and it is the qualification's anchor. Over a period long enough for the department's own year to be visible in the figures, the learner maps what their department actually does, determines which processes are candidates for automation and which are not, costs the options with real numbers from real systems, consults the people whose work would change, sequences the work across a year, builds in the checks and the fallbacks, and presents the plan to whoever signs departmental plans off.

The deliverable is stated in the brief. Almost nothing else is: not which processes, not what counts as a benefit, not what a good sequence looks like, not whose agreement is needed, and not whether the answer is to automate much or little.

Delivery note. The 140 hours are almost entirely the learner's own work, carried out in their job. A departmental planning project run across a year generates continuous, time-stamped, real-work evidence. That is normally the hardest thing to produce for a qualification at this level, and the most expensive for a conventional provider to assess.

Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

LO1

The learner will: Be able to establish what own department does and what it costs.

RefThe learner can
1.1Determine the boundary of the department the plan will cover, and state the reasons for it
1.2Map the processes the department carries out across a year, including those that occur only occasionally
1.3Establish the volume, the time and the cost of each main process from the department's own systems
1.4Determine the method used to establish those figures, and state the reasons for choosing it
1.5Evaluate the reliability of the figures, and state how gaps and disputed figures were handled
1.6Identify the obligations and commitments the department must continue to meet whatever the plan changes
LO2

The learner will: Be able to analyse which of a department's processes are candidates for automation.

RefThe learner can
2.1Determine the criteria by which candidate processes will be selected, and state the reasons for them
2.2Analyse each main process against those criteria
2.3Interpret the cost and the benefit of automating each candidate, using the department's own figures
2.4Use relevant investigation of comparable work to inform the appraisal, and state what it established
2.5Identify the processes that should not be automated, and evaluate the reasons for each
2.6Evaluate the candidates against one another and state the order in which they are recommended
LO3

The learner will: Be able to consult the people whose work a plan would change.

RefThe learner can
3.1Identify the people whose work each recommended change would affect, and how it would affect them
3.2Consult those people, and record what was raised
3.3Analyse the concerns raised, and evaluate which of them change the plan
3.4Adapt the plan in response, and state what changed and the reasons
3.5Evaluate the skills the department will need that it does not currently hold, and state how they will be obtained
LO4

The learner will: Be able to produce a sequenced annual plan with checks and fallbacks.

RefThe learner can
4.1Sequence the recommended changes across the year, and state the reasons for the order
4.2Identify the dependencies between the changes, and between the changes and work outside the department
4.3State the resources each change requires, and when they are required
4.4State the checks that will be carried out on each change, by whom, and at what point
4.5State the fallback for each change, and the point at which the fallback would be used
4.6Evaluate the risks the plan carries for the department, and state what would be done about each
4.7Determine how the effect of each change will be measured, and against what starting figure
LO5

The learner will: Be able to present a departmental plan and evaluate own method.

RefThe learner can
5.1Present the plan to the person who signs off the department's plans, in a form suited to that decision
5.2Respond to challenge on the plan's figures, its sequence and its risks
5.3Record the decision taken and any conditions attached to it
5.4Evaluate how effective the method determined at 1.4 and the criteria determined at 2.1 were, giving reasons
5.5Evaluate how far the consultation changed the plan, giving reasons
5.6Identify what would be determined differently in the next annual plan, and the reasons

Level check

What is given, and what is not

The brief names the deliverable: an annual automation plan for your department. That is the whole of what is stated. It does not name the processes, the criteria, the method, the measure of benefit, the people who must agree, the shape of the year, or the answer. Two competent learners in the same department would produce different plans and both could be sound. That is the descriptor's broadly defined, and it is why the same brief at Level 3 is not the same kind of brief at all. There the object was named and only the instance was chosen.

What would push it to Level 4

Supplying the criteria at 2.1, supplying the process list at 1.2, or supplying a business case template that fixes what counts as a benefit. Any one defines the problem for the learner and drops the unit a level. The assessment guidance says so, because supplying a template is exactly the kind of helpfulness a delivery team adds later without realising what it costs.

What would push it to Level 7

Both exclusions are explicit. First, reframing what the department is for. Second, producing a plan that changes how the organisation as a whole operates. A learner who produces either has produced work above the level, and the criterion is still not met. Assessors find this counter-intuitive and need telling. Where a plan genuinely requires an organisational change to proceed, the correct treatment is to record it at 4.2 as a dependency outside the department, not to widen the plan.

Not Level 6

Nothing asks the learner to critically analyse, to design research, or to evaluate implications. 4.2 identifies dependencies. It does not place the learner in a context where many factors interact and none can be held constant.

Section 6

Assessment and quality assurance.

The same model applies to every unit at both levels.

1

Portfolio of evidence from real work

Assessment is by portfolio drawn from work the learner is already responsible for, gathered over the period of the unit. There is no examination.

2

The AI teaches. A human assessor decides.

Zavmo's AI tutors deliver the learning and prompt the learner to produce and submit evidence. The AI does not make the assessment decision. Every decision on every criterion is made by a named, occupationally competent, appropriately qualified human assessor, internally verified by a qualified IQA against a sampling plan, and sampled by the awarding organisation's External Quality Assurer.

3

How evidence is captured

Learner activity in the platform is captured as xAPI statements and assembled into the portfolio: the tasks they selected, the instruction sets they wrote and rewrote, the outputs the tool returned, the checks they ran, the amendments they made and the reasons they gave. This gives the assessor a time-stamped record of the learner's process rather than only a finished artefact. The xAPI record is evidence presented to the assessor. It is not an assessment decision.

4

Professional discussion is mandatory

At least one recorded professional discussion per unit, conducted by the qualified assessor. This is the primary control for authenticating the learner at distance and confirming the portfolio is their own work.

5

Declared AI use

Learners are expected to use AI in producing evidence, because that is the subject of the qualification. All AI use in evidence must be declared. The assessment decision rests on the learner's judgement about the AI output, not on the polish of the output itself, and that judgement is confirmed in live discussion.

6

Suggested evidence

Records of professional discussion and oral questioning for the knowledge outcomes. Product evidence, annotated by the learner, for the skills outcomes. Witness testimony from a line manager where a criterion turns on something that happened in real work. Reflective accounts for the review outcomes. These are suggestions and centres could use other evidence, provided the criteria are covered and the evidence can be internally and externally verified.

7

Real-work grounding

The tasks used must be tasks the learner is already responsible for. Simulated tasks should only be used where a learner's role genuinely offers no suitable real task, and the reason must be recorded.

Section 7

What we know is not yet right.

Listed plainly, because we would rather you had this list than found it.

Open item 1

The units have not been read by an assessor

They are written to the descriptors and internally checked. Nobody with assessment experience has read them. This is the largest single gap.

Open item 2

The two rewritten units are new and unreviewed

ZFF-GM-05 and ZFF-EI-05 have been rewritten to departmental scope and to the house style of the other three. That answers the previous objection. It does not mean they are right, and they are the two units we would most like a view on.

Open item 3

TQT is allocated, not measured

The unit hours are judgements that produce a defensible total. If any unit's hours are revised down, the Diploma needs re-checking against the 370 hour threshold. This Diploma has 40 hours of headroom.

Open item 4

Whether AI-led tutoring counts as guided learning is unresolved

GLH is shown as indicative throughout. The taught and applied split in section 2 depends on the answer.

Open item 5

How knowledge criteria may be evidenced is unresolved

We have assumed recorded tutoring dialogue can evidence knowledge outcomes rather than written tests. That assumption is favourable to our model, so it should not be taken on our say-so. If written assessment is required, the hours split needs revisiting.

Open item 6

The assessor layer does not exist yet

We do not currently employ occupationally competent assessors or a qualified IQA. The model in section 6 describes what we will put in place, not what is running today.

Open item 7

The Diploma has not been checked against the register

It has not been compared with qualifications already regulated. That check has to happen before submission.

Open item 8

Demand figures are excluded on purpose

We hold internal estimates of which fluencies are most in demand. They are not evidence and they are not in this specification.

Where this stands.

Five units, 410 hours, 41 credits, 136 assessment criteria, every one held against the Ofqual Level 5 descriptor and shown against it. Two units have been rewritten to departmental scope and are marked as such. The open items above are the honest list of what still has to be settled.
