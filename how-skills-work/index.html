How Skills and assessments work

These screens show Skills as we are building it.

Step 1 of 18

What Skills is for

Skills is one record of what each person can do. It builds up across every pathway they take.

That record does two jobs.

  • The next pathway starts where the last one ended. Nobody is taught again what they showed last month.
  • Your people lead can answer the board. Do we have the skills we need? Skills shows the answer, and what it rests on.

It works in three steps. You say which skills matter. We find out where each person is. Then you act on the gap, and every action adds to the record.

Skills opens on the Overview. Every screen in Skills ends in something to do.

Step 2 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

What to do next

The Overview opens with a short list of cards. Zavmo writes them, most useful first.

Each card is one line and one button. It says why, and what the figure rests on.

  • A team below target on a priority skill: give them a pathway.
  • A priority skill no pathway builds: ask Zavmo to draft one.
  • A role profile due for review: review it.
  • Managers with levels waiting: remind them.

Nothing changes until you press the button. You can set any card aside.

Step 3 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

The skills picture

Below the cards sits the skills picture. It shows the whole organisation, or any department, location, role or team.

It leads with your priority skills. These are the few skills your plans depend on, each with a target.

Each skill has one bar of people, in four parts:

  • Shown at work at the target.
  • Rated at the target, but not yet shown at work.
  • Below the target.
  • No rating yet.

Every figure says what it rests on, and the date of the newest rating. A figure with its source gets used. One without gets argued about.

Download for the board gives one page with your priority skills and what changed.

Step 4 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

Role profiles from our library

A role profile says which skills a role needs, and how well. Without one there is no gap, only a rating.

You do not need to write role profiles or job descriptions from scratch.

Search our job description library for the role, for example "Operations team lead". We built it from over 43,000 job descriptions.

Pick the closest one. Zavmo drafts the profile from it, with the essential skills and a target for each.

Have your own job description? Drop it in and Zavmo refines the draft. That is optional.

Nothing is live until you approve it. Each profile has a named owner and a review date.

Step 5 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

A role's gaps

Open a role to see its gaps. Essential skills come first, each with the same four-part bar.

Each skill names the pathway that closes it. One button gives each person the pathway for the skill they are below on.

Ready for this role lists people in other roles who already show the essentials, or miss by one. That is where succession starts.

When the job description behind a profile changes, Zavmo proposes a change for you to check.

Step 6 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

Who can do this?

A project, a move or a gap in a team all start with one question: who can do this?

Pick a skill and the lowest level you need. Narrow it by department, location or role. By default it shows only people who have shown it at work.

Each result says how the level is known, and when. Add people to a shortlist and download it.

Nothing is sent to anyone from this screen. You see only the people you are allowed to see.

Step 7 of 18 · Your organisation's skills

A person's skills record

Each person has one skills record. It shows their skills against the profile for their role.

Every rating sits side by side, with who gave it and when. We never average them into one number.

The level that counts is the one shown at work. The person sees every rating, and who gave it.

The record ends in a next step: a pathway, a stretch task at work, or a talk with their manager.

Step 8 of 18 · Assessments

What an assessment is for

An assessment finds out where someone is on a skill. Each answer adds a rating to their record.

So a person is asked once, not on every pathway. A pathway asks only about skills the record does not already hold.

The same assessment can be used at three moments.

  1. At the startPlace themIt places each learner, so they skip what they can already do.
  2. At the endShow the changeThe same skills are asked again, so you can see what moved.
  3. A month laterCheck it stuckThe learner and their manager rate again, once it has been used at work.

Step 9 of 18 · Assessments

Building one from your skills

You build an assessment in Skills, from the skills in your framework. You do not write questions from scratch.

Ask Zavmo, for example: build a starting assessment for team leads, from their profile.

Zavmo takes the skills from the profile and drafts the questions. It writes one line on what each level looks like.

Anything Zavmo wrote says To check until you have read it. You publish once you have checked it.

Step 10 of 18 · Assessments

Practice and confidence

We ask about practice, not knowledge. "Have you done this at work?" is a question people answer well.

The level lives in the skill itself: in their own work, across a team, a department or the whole organisation. A learner never sees a number.

PracticeKnows it, has applied it, uses it routinely.
ConfidenceNot confident, fairly confident, confident.

Confidence sits beside the answer and is never turned into a level. High confidence with little practice is where someone needs support most.

Step 11 of 18 · Assessments

Who rates

An assessment can have more than one rater.

  • The learner rates themselves, at the start and at the end.
  • Their manager confirms the level Zavmo proposes from their work.
  • Two quick questions come before anyone skips a lesson. They show the person knows it, never that they use it.

Self-ratings are a good start, and a poor proof. People new to a skill often rate themselves high. So the level that counts is the one shown at work.

Step 12 of 18 · Assessments

Managers confirm a level

Managers skip blank forms. So we never send one.

Instead, Zavmo proposes a level from what the person did: practice tasks, work signed off and their own rating. The manager agrees, or picks the level they have seen.

It comes a few skills at a time, at the end of a pathway and at the follow-up. It takes about a minute.

The answer is a rating from the manager, with the date. The person sees it, with their name.

Step 13 of 18 · Assessments

At the start and at the end

At the start of a pathway, an assessment places each learner. They skip what they can already do.

A skip is "placed", never "shown". It counts as shown only once they do it at work.

At the end, the same skills are asked again. You see the start, the end and what was shown at work, side by side. We never combine them into one score.

A month later, the learner and their manager rate the same skills again.

Step 14 of 18 · Assessments

How a result lands in the record

Every answer becomes a rating in the person's record: who gave it, the level, their confidence and the date.

A rating is never edited. A new one is added beside it, so you can see how a skill moved over time.

The next pathway reads the record first. It asks only about skills with no recent rating.

Step 15 of 18 · Frameworks and pathways

Your skills framework

A skills framework lists the skills your organisation asks for. It says what each one looks like at each level.

You have one main framework. Role profiles and assessments draw from it, and from ours. Any other framework is matched onto it, so ratings can be compared.

Each level carries one line on what it looks like at work. Every rater sees the same lines.

The framework has a named owner and a review date. When a job description changes, Zavmo proposes a change.

Step 16 of 18 · Frameworks and pathways

Skills in your pathways

This is the join between learning and the record. Each module names the skills it builds.

Without that link, a pathway cannot skip what someone has shown, and cannot record what moved.

Zavmo reads a pathway's outcomes and suggests the skills. You check them.

A pathway with no skills linked adds nothing to anyone's record, so Skills tells you.

Step 17 of 18 · Frameworks and pathways

How Discover and Demonstrate feed Skills

Two stages of every pathway write to Skills.

  • Discover runs the starting assessment. It places each learner, so they skip what they can already do.
  • Demonstrate asks for evidence from their own work. When it is signed off, the skill is recorded as shown at work.

The pathway walkthrough shows both stages, screen by screen.

See Discover and see Demonstrate in how a Zavmo pathway works.
