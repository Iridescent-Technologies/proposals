Section one — the pedagogical breakthrough

How Zavmo solves Bloom's 2 Sigma Problem

In 1984, the American educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom showed that learners who received one-to-one tutoring performed two standard deviations better than classroom peers — the average tutored student outperformed 98% of those in a conventional classroom. The challenge ever since has been the same: tutoring works, but no economy can afford a tutor for every learner. Zavmo closes that gap.

Tutoring at the scale of a country

Bloom's challenge — to find affordable group instruction that matches one-to-one tutoring — was unsolvable in the classroom era. Generative AI has changed the economics. A modern adaptive system can hold a personalised conversation with every learner, simultaneously, around the clock.

Zavmo's pilot data already shows the pattern: 85% completion against the 15–35% industry average, 150% better retention through spaced repetition, and 40% less time to competency than traditional training. These outcomes are not marketing — they are the signature of a system closing the sigma gap.

The average tutored learner outperformed 98% of those in conventional classrooms (Bloom, 1984). The work of Zavmo is to deliver that, in Armenian, to every learner who needs it.

Section four — real-time adaption

Making learning as unique as you are

Personalisation in Zavmo is not a profile you fill in once. It is a moment-by-moment adjustment to how a learner is actually behaving — the language they reach for, the load they can carry, the way a conversation moves them forward, and ultimately, whether their behaviour changes in the workplace. Four established models, working together, do the adapting.

Model 01

Growth Mindset

Carol Dweck (2006) — Mindset: The New Psychology of Success

Dweck's research shows that learners who believe ability is malleable persist longer, take on harder challenges, and recover faster from setbacks. The opposite belief — that ability is fixed — causes learners to avoid difficulty altogether.

In Zavmo: the platform watches the language a learner uses about themselves. "I can't" triggers a different conversational move than "I'm not sure yet." Effort, strategy and persistence are surfaced and praised, never raw ability.
Model 02

Conversational learning

Vygotsky (1978), Zone of Proximal Development

Learning happens in dialogue, in the zone between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with a knowledgeable other. Real understanding is built by being asked, prompted and stretched in conversation — not by passive consumption.

In Zavmo: every lesson is a conversation with a tutor character calibrated to the learner. Difficulty is tuned in real time so the learner is always stretched, never stranded.
Model 03

Cognitive Load Theory

Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas (2019)

Working memory has limits. When intrinsic load (the difficulty of the material) and extraneous load (poor design) exceed capacity, learning fails. Effective design protects germane load — the effortful, productive thinking that builds schemas.

In Zavmo: attention, response time and hesitation patterns are monitored continuously. When load spikes, the platform paces, simplifies or switches modality before the learner is overwhelmed.
Model 04

Kirkpatrick — outcomes, not delivery

Kirkpatrick & Kirkpatrick (2016) — Four Levels of Training Evaluation

Most learning systems measure Level 1 (did the learner enjoy it?) and Level 2 (can they pass a test?). Kirkpatrick's framework insists on Level 3 (does behaviour change at work?) and Level 4 (do business results improve?). Anything less is measuring delivery, not learning.

In Zavmo: xAPI tracking and the Mind Palace evidence model capture L3 and L4 — the behaviours that show up in the workplace and the outcomes that show up in the organisation.

For a ministry or employer, this is the only measurement that matters. Zavmo can show, by sector and by cohort, whether learners are doing differently in their work, not merely whether they have consumed content. That is the difference between a training cost and a national capability investment.
