Level 3 and Level 5 Diplomas · learner briefing

You are going to be graded on the work you are already doing.

This is the Future Fluency Diploma, at Level 3 and at Level 5. It is not a course you sit through. It is a record of what you can do, built out of your own job, and signed off by a human assessor.

Why we graded it

Training you finish is a tick. A qualification you earn is portable.

We could have built Future Fluency as a course. Courses end with a completion record, and a completion record only means you were present. It does not travel to your next role, and it does not tell anyone what you can actually do.

So we levelled it instead. Every learning outcome in these two Diplomas is written against the Ofqual descriptors for its level, and every criterion is checked against them. That is what makes the difference between saying you are good with AI and having it assessed.

The evidence is your own work. The qualification is what happens when someone independent agrees.

A note on wording. These are customised qualifications carrying a level equivalence, benchmarked against the Ofqual Level 3 and Level 5 descriptors. That is not the same as an Ofqual-regulated listing, and we do not claim it is. Centre approval with the awarding organisation is in progress.

What it actually does

It takes the two years everyone is worried about and makes them countable.

The subject

Working with AI, data, automation and the governance around all three. Plus the two human ones: keeping your skills current, and handling the people whose roles change.

The method

You do it inside your real job. Every unit ends with something you produced at work, not an essay about producing it.

The output

A portfolio, a recorded professional discussion per unit, and a level. Assessed by a qualified human, internally verified, then sampled externally.

Which one you are on

The difference between Level 3 and Level 5 is scope, not difficulty.

This is the thing worth getting straight before anything else, because it decides which units you get.

3

Level 3 stays with you

You answer a question about your own work. You automate a task in your own work. The moment an outcome reaches a team or a department, it has left Level 3. Problems here are well defined, and may still be complex and non-routine.

5

Level 5 reaches a department

You plan automation across a department, and you direct its priorities with data. Problems here are broadly defined, which means part of your job is working out what the problem is. You also lead the people whose roles change because of it.

Length does not set the level. A long project on a well-defined problem is still Level 3. Only the definition of the problem sets the level.

How Zavmo works

Four steps, and the fourth one is why the hours count.

Zavmo runs every journey through the same loop. What matters here is that the loop was built to produce evidence, not to produce completions.

1Discover

Work out where you actually are

Zavmo compares what you can already do against what the unit requires. You skip what you have, so the 390 hours are not spent proving things twice.

Sets your starting point
2Discuss

Agree the route

You and Zavmo settle the path through the units, against your real role and sector. Your manager can see it. Every criterion is tickable before you start.

Names the criteria up front
3Deliver

Learn by talking, not by reading slides

You learn in conversation with a tutor that adapts to your pace. It keeps pushing you up from remembering toward evaluating and creating, which is where the level lives.

Climbs the thinking ladder
4Demonstrate

End with proof, not a completion tick

Your tasks, reflections and real examples are captured and mapped to criteria as you go. You are not assembling a portfolio at the end. It builds while you work.

Becomes your portfolio

One thing to be straight about. Zavmo teaches and it prompts you for evidence. It does not decide whether you passed. Every criterion is decided by a named human assessor, internally verified, and sampled by the awarding organisation.

Applying it

What each unit asks you to actually go and do.

This is the part that makes the time pay back. Every unit ends in something real, produced at work, that exists whether or not you were studying.

Level 3. Everything stays inside your own work.

Six units, six things you will have made by the end.

ZFF-AI-03

An AI output you checked, with the check on record

Use a tool on a real task. Show what you verified, what you changed, and where the tool was wrong. Declaring your AI use is part of the criteria, not a confession.

ZFF-GM-03

A skill you rebuilt because the work moved

Pick something in your role that changed. Show how you learned it, and review honestly how well the method worked.

ZFF-DA-03

A recommendation put to the person who decides

Take a question about your own work. Choose the data, say what you left out and why, then present it and answer the challenge back.

ZFF-GV-03

Your own work, made safe to do with AI

Where the data goes, what you are not allowed to put in, what to do when the tool gets it wrong. Applied to your job, not to a policy in general.

ZFF-AU-03

One repeated task, automated

Find something you do every week. Automate it, run it, and show what it now costs you instead.

ZFF-PJ-03

The applied project. A hundred hours of your real job

Improve your own work with AI, end to end. Ten guided hours out of a hundred. This is the unit the assessor will spend the longest on.

Level 5. Everything reaches a department.

Five units, and three of them involve other people's time.

ZFF-GM-05

A department taken through a capability change

Work out what your people will need to be able to do next. Then lead the shift, and evaluate whether your approach worked.

ZFF-EI-05

The conversations with people whose roles change

You will consult them, tell each of them what is expected, and say what support exists. Witness testimony carries this, so plan the meetings.

ZFF-AU-05

An automation plan across the department

Not one task. The set of them, sequenced, with the trade-offs stated and the reasoning behind the order you chose.

ZFF-DA-05

Priorities directed by data, and defended

Analyse, interpret and evaluate. Present it to a real decision maker, with the alternative reading of the same figures on the table.

ZFF-PJ-05

The applied project. Your department's annual automation plan

A hundred and forty hours, ten of them guided. This is a document your organisation could act on, and that is the standard it is held to.

The return

What you get for the hours.

  • A level that travels. It is benchmarked against the national descriptors, so it means something outside this company.
  • Work you would have done anyway, counted. The project units alone are a quarter to a third of the qualification, and they are your own job.
  • Automation you keep. Whatever you automate in the applied units stays automated after the assessment.
  • A defensible answer on AI. You will have evidence that you check outputs, declare your use, and know what not to put into a tool.
  • A record built as you go. No scramble at the end to assemble a portfolio you should have been keeping.

What we are not promising: a pay rise, a promotion, or a percentage figure for productivity. Anyone quoting you one of those has made it up.

Being straight with you

Three things worth knowing before you say yes.

1

The hours are allocated, not measured

390 and 410 are our estimates of the time a typical learner needs. Nobody has run a full cohort yet. If it turns out to be wrong, we will say so and change the figure.

2

Some criteria need other people

At Level 5 in particular, a few criteria depend on a colleague or a manager giving you their time. Those are the ones that stall. Get them in the diary in the first month.

3

A human decides, and a human can say no

Every criterion is judged by a qualified assessor and sampled externally. That is what makes it worth having. It also means the AI cannot pass you.

Pick your level, then pick your project.

The project unit is the largest thing in either Diploma. Choosing it well is the single decision that makes the rest of the hours worth spending. Bring one idea to the next session.
