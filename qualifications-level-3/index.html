Section 1

What Future Fluency is

Future Fluency is about working well alongside AI and automation. Not building the technology. Using it, checking it, and leading a team through what it changes.

The subject moves as the level rises, and it moves deliberately.

  • At Level 3 the learner changes their own daily work. They use AI tools on real tasks, check what comes back, automate something they repeat, and review what changed.
  • At Level 5 the learner leads a department through the same shift. Technical depth does not rise. Judgement, analysis and working with people does.

This matters for reviewing the specifications. A reader expecting deeper AI content at Level 5 will think the units have gone soft. They have not. The level rises through the complexity of the problem, not the difficulty of the tool.

This page specifies the Level 3 qualifications. The Level 5 Diploma is specified in the same pack and is not reproduced here.

The share of guided learning falls as the level rises, and that is the descriptor doing its work. A Level 5 problem is only broadly defined. A large part of what the learner needs cannot be known in advance by anyone, including the tutor. The department, its systems and its people are the content, and they are found in the doing.

Two things are easy to misread in that table.

Guided learning is not the same as time with Zavmo. It is the hours spent under direction. Tutoring is one part of that, not all of it.

The other hours are not all project work. They are reflection, reading and personal investigation as well as the project, and most of it happens inside the learner's own job.

Section 3

The Level 3 units

The level rule these six are written to

Every criterion is held against the Ofqual RQF Level 3 descriptor: problems that, while well defined, may be complex and non-routine; the learner can interpret and evaluate relevant information and ideas, is aware of different perspectives or approaches, uses appropriate investigation to inform actions, and reviews how effective methods and actions have been.

Deliberately absent throughout: analyse (Level 4), critically analyse and design (Level 6), reformulate (Level 7).

Scope stays personal in every unit. The moment an outcome reaches a colleague, a team or a department, it has left Level 3.

Evidence

The xAPI evidence trail

An assessor working at a distance from the learner has one hard problem. The portfolio arrives finished, and nothing in a finished artefact proves who made it, in what order, or whether the learner's account of producing it is true. Zavmo records what a learner did in the platform as time-stamped statements, and those statements can be put in front of the assessor alongside the portfolio.

This section sets out what that record holds, how a single statement is tied to a learning outcome and an assessment criterion, and what the record cannot show. Each unit then carries the same three things for its own outcomes.

Where the record lives

Zavmo uses the xAPI vocabulary of actor, verb, object, context and result, but there is no external learning record store. Every statement is a row in one Postgres table inside the Zavmo API service. There is one store and nothing is mirrored outside the platform. For an awarding organisation that matters, because retention, access control and export of learner records are then properties of one system rather than of a chain of them.

How a statement is tied to a criterion

Every statement built through Zavmo's statement builder carries ten mandatory context extensions. Two of them are the ones that matter to a regulated qualification:

  • http://zavmo.ai/extensions/learning_outcome_id, the learning outcome the activity was against
  • http://zavmo.ai/extensions/assessment_criteria_addressed, the list of assessment criteria the activity bore on

Both are required arguments of the builder. A statement cannot be constructed without them, so there is no route by which activity is recorded but left unattached to an outcome. When the platform records that a learning outcome has been met, the statement carries the outcome reference and the criteria references with it. That pair of extensions is the bridge between a time-stamped event and a line in a unit on this page.

Catalogued, wired, observed

Three numbers describe the event catalogue and they mean three different things. Reading them as one number would give an inspector a false picture of what is running, so every event named on this page carries which of the three it is.

217

StatusWhat it meansWhat an assessor should do with it
ObservedThe event has fired in the production system and the statements exist.Treat as available evidence, subject to the limits below.

150 catalogued events have never fired. An event that has never emitted is not evidence, and we would rather say so here than have it found in an external quality assurance visit. The gap between wired and observed is emitters sitting on code paths that production learners have not yet taken.

Counts measured on 17 July 2026. The catalogued and wired figures come from an audit that is regenerated from the code itself and fails the build if it drifts from it. The observed figure is a read only aggregate over the production statement table, covering 20,610 statements from 547 distinct learners between 13 February 2026 and 17 July 2026.

What the record cannot do

1

Roughly four statements in five carry no canonical event identifier. In the measured window only 19% of rows carried one. The rest are real activity recorded through the front-end path without a registry identifier, so they cannot be attributed to a named event. They are still statements, and they still carry a learner, a verb, an object and a time. They are simply not countable by event.

2

The two extensions are populated from Zavmo's own identifiers, not from Gateway's. Today the outcome and criteria slots are filled from the learner's own outcome record inside the platform. They do not yet carry references in the form used on this page, such as ZFF-AI-03 3.3. Mapping these units into those slots is work to be done before first registration.

Until it is done, the tie between a statement and a criterion on this page is a mapping we intend rather than one the data already carries. The mechanism is built and proven. The mapping to these six units is not.

3

A status is a measurement with a date on it. Eight events that were wired in July are not any more, because the feature that emitted them was deliberately removed. Two of those had already fired, so they stay in the observed snapshot permanently. A further six event identifiers have fired in production that are not in the registry at all. No status should be read without its date, and none of those retired or unregistered events is referenced in the unit sections below.

4

xAPI records the platform, not the job. It has no sight of what a learner did in a spreadsheet, a workflow tool, a meeting or a conversation with their manager. At Level 3 most of the assessed activity is in the job, so for most criteria the platform record is corroboration around workplace evidence rather than the evidence itself. The unit sections say plainly where that line falls.

What this is, and what it is not

The AI teaches. A human assessor decides. The xAPI record is evidence put in front of the assessor. It is never an assessment decision. No criterion in this specification is met or not met because a statement exists, and where the platform runs its own validation checks those checks are formative: they inform the assessor and decide nothing. Every decision on every criterion is made by the named human assessor, internally verified, and sampled externally.

Each unit lists the events most likely to bear on its outcomes. The record for a learner is not limited to those events.

ZFF-AI-03

Using and Checking AI Tools in Daily Work

Level3TQT70 hoursCredits7GLH35 indicativeStatusmandatoryCriteria28

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to use everyday AI tools on the tasks they already carry out in their own job, and to check what those tools produce before it is used. Learners find out what a large language model does when it answers a question, and why it can be wrong while reading as certain. They then select real tasks from their own working week, write and improve the instructions they give the tool, check the output against other sources, correct it, and review what has changed about the way they work. Scope is personal. All evidence comes from work the learner is already responsible for.

What xAPI does not evidence here

The whole of LO2 sits outside the platform. Nothing in the record shows that the task selected at 2.1 is genuinely one the learner is responsible for, or that the information identified at 2.3 is what their employer actually restricts.

The two sources used to check an output (4.3), the errors found in it (4.4) and the reasons for each amendment (4.6) exist as the learner's work, not as platform activity, unless the learner submits them. The record cannot show that the amended output was used in the job (5.1), that the time estimate at 5.2 is sound, or that AI use was declared in line with the employer's rules (5.4).

Those need product evidence annotated by the learner, witness testimony from the line manager for 5.1 and 5.4, and the recorded professional discussion for LO1 and 4.7.

ZFF-GM-03

Developing a Growth Mindset for Changing Work

Level3TQT50 hoursCredits5GLH25 indicativeStatusmandatoryCriteria22

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to keep their own working practices up to date as AI and automation change what their job involves. Learners describe what is changing in the tasks they carry out, compare that with the skills they hold, plan and carry out one piece of their own learning, apply the new skill to a real task, seek and act on feedback, and review what it changed.

A design note that is load-bearing: this unit assesses what the learner does, not the attitude they report holding. Every criterion is an observable action or a description of a real event. No criterion asks the learner to demonstrate that they hold a growth mindset, or asks an assessor to judge their attitude, resilience or confidence. Those would not be assessable. The mindset is what the unit builds. The actions are what it assesses.

What xAPI does not evidence here

Almost all of LO3 and LO4 happens away from the platform. Carrying out the actions in the plan (3.2), using the new skill on a real task (4.1), asking a person who saw the result for feedback (4.3) and changing working practice in response (4.4) are workplace acts and leave no statement.

The platform can show that a plan was submitted. It cannot show that the actions in it were carried out, that the dates in it were met, or that the difficulties described at 3.3 are the ones that actually arose.

The assessor needs the plan itself as product evidence, witness testimony from the person who gave the feedback at 4.3, and professional discussion for 4.5 and the whole of LO5. Because the mindset signals are unusable here by design, this unit leans on discussion more than the others in the Award.

This unit gives the learner the skills to take the data that already exists in their own work and turn it into a recommendation that someone will act on. The emphasis is on the telling rather than the crunching. Learners find out what the figures they see every week actually measure, what they leave out, and how the same numbers can support more than one reading. They then select a question from their own role, gather the data that bears on it, work out what it indicates, and put a recommendation to the person who decides. Scope is personal.

Working Safely and Responsibly with AI

Level check

This is the unit most at risk of drifting above Level 3, because extended project work invites higher-order language. Three things hold it down.

The verbs stay at level. Establish, record, identify, select, state, carry out, interpret, evaluate, review. Not analyse (4), not design or critically evaluate (6), not reformulate (7).

Section 6

Assessment and quality assurance

The same model applies to every unit.

Portfolio of evidence from real work. Assessment is by portfolio drawn from work the learner is already responsible for, gathered over the period of the unit. There is no examination.

The AI teaches. A human assessor decides. Zavmo's AI tutors deliver the learning and prompt the learner to produce and submit evidence. The AI does not make the assessment decision. Every decision on every criterion is made by a named, occupationally competent, appropriately qualified human assessor, internally verified by a qualified IQA against a sampling plan, and sampled by Gateway's External Quality Assurer.

How evidence is captured. Learner activity in the platform is captured as xAPI statements and assembled into the portfolio: the tasks they selected, the instruction sets they wrote and rewrote, the outputs the tool returned, the checks they ran, the amendments they made and the reasons they gave. This gives the assessor a time-stamped record of the learner's process rather than only a finished artefact. That is unusually strong evidence for criteria such as ZFF-AI-03 3.3 and 4.6. The xAPI record is evidence presented to the assessor. It is not an assessment decision.

Professional discussion is mandatory. At least one recorded professional discussion per unit, conducted by the qualified assessor. This is the primary control for authenticating the learner at distance and confirming the portfolio is their own work.

Declared AI use. Learners are expected to use AI in producing evidence, because that is the subject of the qualification. All AI use in evidence must be declared. The assessment decision rests on the learner's judgement about the AI output, not on the polish of the output itself, and that judgement is confirmed in live discussion.

Suggested evidence. Records of professional discussion and oral questioning for the knowledge outcomes. Product evidence, annotated by the learner, for the skills outcomes. Witness testimony from a line manager where a criterion turns on something that happened in real work. Reflective accounts for the review outcomes. These are suggestions and centres could use other evidence, provided the criteria are covered and the evidence can be internally and externally verified.

Real-work grounding. The tasks used must be tasks the learner is already responsible for. Simulated tasks should only be used where a learner's role genuinely offers no suitable real task, and the reason must be recorded.

Section 7

What we know is not yet right

Listed plainly, because we would rather you had this list than found it. These are the items from the full pack that bear on Level 3. One further item in the pack concerns two Level 5 units and is not repeated here.

1

Nine of the eleven units are drafted but not reviewed by an assessor. They are written to the descriptors and internally checked. Nobody with assessment experience has read them. This is the largest single gap.

2

TQT is allocated, not measured. The unit hours are judgements that produce a defensible total. If any unit's hours are revised down, both Diplomas need re-checking against the 370 threshold. The Level 3 Diploma has only 20 hours of headroom.

3

Whether AI-led tutoring counts as guided learning is unresolved. We have shown GLH as indicative throughout. The taught and applied split in section 2.4 depends on the answer and we would like Gateway's view.

4

Whether knowledge criteria can be evidenced through recorded tutoring dialogue rather than written tests is unresolved. We have assumed they can, which is favourable to our model, so it should not be taken on our say-so. If written assessment is required for knowledge outcomes, section 2.4 needs revisiting.

5

The assessor layer does not exist yet. We do not currently employ occupationally competent assessors or a qualified IQA. The model in section 6 describes what we will put in place, not what is running today. We would like to talk about the sequence.

6

The two Diplomas have not been checked against qualifications already on the register. ZFF-DA-03 is the unit most likely to overlap something existing.

7

Demand figures are excluded on purpose. We hold internal estimates of which fluencies are most in demand. They are not evidence and they are not in this document.

Assessor readiness

What the readiness pass found

All 145 criteria were tested on four questions. Could an assessor see whether the criterion was met, or does it require judging an attitude. Is it at Level 3 against the descriptor in section 3. Does it contradict anything the unit itself states. Could a learner in a real job produce evidence for it.

No criterion has been changed. Every item below is a finding and a proposal, for the assessor of record to accept, amend or reject. Two of them were already known and are repeated so that the list is complete: the guided learning share in ZFF-GV-03, and the condensed unit aims.

Chips show what each proposal would require. Change proposed means an edit to the specification. Guidance only means the criteria stand and the assessment guidance carries the answer. No change means it was tested and it holds.

1

The scope rule in section 3 is stated absolutely, and three units cross it. Section 3 says that the moment an outcome reaches a colleague, a team or a department, it has left Level 3. But ZFF-DA-03 4.2, 4.4 and 4.5 require the learner to present a recommendation to the person who decides and answer a challenge to it, ZFF-PJ-03 5.4 and 5.5 recommend to the learner's manager, and ZFF-GM-03 4.3 requires feedback from a person who saw the result. Those are right, and the rule as written appears to forbid them.

The rule is really about whose work is being changed, not about who is in the room. As stated it does not say so, and that is the kind of internal contradiction an external quality assurer reads first.

Change proposed Reword the section 3 sentence to separate the two: the work under improvement stays the learner's own, while the audience for a recommendation or a piece of feedback may be another person. No criterion changes.

2

ZFF-GV-03 gives 60% of its hours to guided learning, against 50% or less everywhere else. The unit shows 30 guided hours against 50 TQT. The comparison across the Diploma is ZFF-AI-03 at 50%, ZFF-GM-03 at 50%, ZFF-DA-03 at 50%, ZFF-AU-03 at 42% and ZFF-PJ-03 at 10%. ZFF-GV-03 is the only unit above half, and it is a unit whose skills outcomes are almost entirely workplace conduct, which argues for fewer guided hours rather than more.

Change proposed Reduce to 25 guided hours, matching the other 50 TQT unit. The knock-on is small and needs to be carried through: the Diploma total falls from 155 to 150 guided hours in the unit index and in section 2.4, and the guided share in section 2.4 moves from 40% to 38%. TQT, credits and the Award are unaffected.

3

Some unit aims were condensed and lost their trailing scope clause. ZFF-DA-03's aim now ends "Scope is personal." The fuller original ended by saying what the recommendation is not about, namely a department's performance. ZFF-GM-03 and ZFF-PJ-03 carry no scope sentence in their aims at all, while ZFF-AI-03, ZFF-GV-03 and ZFF-AU-03 each carry a full one.

Scope is what holds these units at Level 3. The aim is the first thing a reviewer reads and the last thing a centre reads before writing an assessment plan, so it is the wrong place to be terse.

Change proposed Reinstate the fuller wording in ZFF-DA-03, and add matching clauses to ZFF-GM-03 and ZFF-PJ-03 so that all six aims state the boundary in the same shape.

4

ZFF-PJ-03's aim uses a verb its own level check rules out. The aim says the learner "redesigns" the process. The same unit's level check states that the verbs stay at level and that design is not among them, and ZFF-AU-03's level check says plainly that design is a Level 6 verb and using it would mis-level the unit. No criterion uses it, so this is confined to the aim, but it is the sentence a reviewer reads before the criteria.

Change proposed Replace "redesigns it" with "changes it" or "sets up an improved version of it". ZFF-AU-03's aim also contains "designed to break it", which describes the test cases rather than an act of the learner; that one is defensible and we suggest leaving it.

5

Nine criteria presuppose an event that may not happen. They are assessable when the event occurs and unachievable when it does not, and nothing currently tells a centre what to do in the second case.

ZFF-GM-03 4.3 needs a person willing to give feedback. ZFF-DA-03 5.1 needs a decision to be made and communicated. ZFF-GV-03 3.5 needs a situation where the rules did not clearly apply, 4.4 needs a response to a reported risk, and 5.2 is worded so that the learner's practice must have fallen short. ZFF-AU-03 4.2 and 4.3 need the automation to produce a wrong result. ZFF-PJ-03 4.1 needs a problem to arise and 4.5 needs one to remain unresolved.

Two of the unit aims lean into this, saying the learner runs the automation until it is shown reliable and runs the new process until it fails at least once. A qualification should not depend on a failure arriving to order.

Guidance only The criteria are sound. Add a nil case to the assessment guidance for each: what the learner records instead, and how the assessor tests the same underlying skill. For ZFF-AU-03 the fallback is already present in the unit, since 3.2 requires test cases expected to fail.

6

ZFF-AI-03 4.1 is a knowledge criterion under a skills outcome. LO4 reads "Be able to interpret and evaluate the output of an AI tool", and 4.1 asks the learner to describe what critical thinking means when applied to an AI output. Describing a concept is knowledge. The other six criteria in LO4 are all acts on a real output.

It is a small thing and it is exactly the kind of small thing an external quality assurer picks up, because the convention that "be able to" outcomes carry skills criteria is well established.

Change proposed Move it into LO1 as a seventh criterion, where the other knowledge sits. The alternative, leaving it and noting the reason in the assessment guidance, is defensible but weaker.

7

ZFF-GM-03 LO2's stem verb is not discharged by any of its criteria. The outcome is "Be able to compare own current skills with the skills own role will require", and its four criteria ask the learner to identify strengths, identify missing skills, select one and identify sources of help. None of them asks for a comparison. The comparing happens at 5.1, under LO5.

The same pattern appears in a milder form at ZFF-PJ-03 LO2, where no criterion says "plan", although 2.1 to 2.6 taken together plainly constitute planning. We would leave that one.

Change proposed Either restem LO2 to match what its criteria do, for example "Be able to identify the skills own role will require", or add a comparison criterion. Restemming is the smaller change and does not touch a criterion.

8

ZFF-AI-03's level check claims more than its criteria support. It maps the descriptor clause on being aware of different perspectives or approaches to "1.1 to 1.3, and 1.6". Criteria 1.1 to 1.3 describe how a language model produces answers and why it can be wrong. That is the nature of the area, not a set of perspectives, and 1.1 and 1.2 are already claimed against that clause in the row above. Only 1.6 genuinely answers it.

Change proposed Narrow the mapping to 1.6, and add 2.2 if a second reference is wanted, since identifying a task that should not be given to an AI tool and giving reasons carries a competing approach. The claim is weaker and it is true.

9

ZFF-GM-03's level check maps "interpret and evaluate" to a criterion that reviews. The check cites 5.2, whose wording is "Review how far the actions taken changed own performance of a task in own role". Review is a different descriptor clause, and LO5 is already claimed against it in the same sentence. Nothing in the unit uses the verb evaluate.

This is the same class of problem as the one already known on the Level 5 page, where a verb is used in one place and ruled out in another. Here it is a mapping the wording does not carry.

Change proposed Cite 5.1, which compares skills at the end against those identified at the start, and 4.4, where feedback is weighed and acted on. If neither is thought sufficient, the honest course is to say the clause is met at qualification level rather than in this unit.

10

ZFF-GM-03's level check does not map "use appropriate investigation to inform actions" at all. Every other unit maps it: ZFF-AI-03 at 4.3 and 4.4, ZFF-DA-03 at 3.2, ZFF-GV-03 at 4.2, ZFF-AU-03 at 4.3 and ZFF-PJ-03 at 4.2. ZFF-GM-03 is one of the two Award units, so between them ZFF-AI-03 and ZFF-GM-03 must carry the descriptor for the Award, and the omission is visible.

Change proposed Cite 2.4, which requires the learner to identify sources of help inside and outside their workplace. That is investigation informing an action, and it needs no criterion change.

11

ZFF-AU-03's level check does not map "aware of different perspectives or approaches". The unit does contain the material: 1.1 and 1.2 set out what makes a task suitable and unsuitable for automation, and 2.4 requires reasons for the steps selected. The clause is simply not claimed.

A descriptor clause does not have to be met by every unit individually, only by the qualification. If that is the intended reading it should be stated, because at present five units map the clause and one silently does not.

Change proposed Either cite 1.1 and 1.2 in the level check, or add a line to section 3 saying the descriptor is evidenced across the qualification rather than unit by unit.

12

Four criteria repeat in substance across units, and one learner could satisfy them all with one piece of evidence. The "different views held by people in own area of work" criterion appears three times, at ZFF-AI-03 1.6, ZFF-GM-03 1.4 and ZFF-GV-03 2.4. The restricted information criterion appears at ZFF-AI-03 2.3 and again at ZFF-GV-03 1.2. Declaring AI use appears at ZFF-AI-03 5.4 and again at ZFF-GV-03 1.5 and 3.3. What happens to information entered into a tool appears at ZFF-AI-03 1.5 and ZFF-GV-03 2.2.

Each is properly placed in its own unit and the subject differs slightly each time. The risk is not the duplication, it is a portfolio in which the same paragraph is submitted three times and passed three times.

Guidance only Keep the criteria. State in the assessment guidance that the same piece of evidence may not be counted against more than one of these, and say what the distinct focus of each is.

13

ZFF-GV-03 4.1 and 4.2 do not agree about whether something has happened. 4.1 asks the learner to identify a risk arising from the use of an AI tool, which reads as prospective. 4.2 asks them to use appropriate investigation to establish what happened, which reads as an incident after the fact. A risk that has not materialised has no "what happened", and a learner will be told two different things by the two criteria.

Change proposed Align 4.2 to the prospective reading at the next revision, for example establishing how the risk arises and what it could affect. Until then, guidance should say that either reading is acceptable and the assessor should record which one the learner took.

14

Two units measure time to different standards. ZFF-AU-03 2.5 and 5.1 both require the learner to state the time a task takes "and how that was established". ZFF-PJ-03 1.3 and 5.1 do the same. ZFF-AI-03 5.2 asks the learner to "estimate the time added or saved" with no basis clause at all, which makes it the one time criterion in the Diploma that an assessor cannot test.

ZFF-AI-03 5.2 also sits in the Award, so it is likely to be the first time criterion any learner meets.

Change proposed Add the same basis clause to ZFF-AI-03 5.2 at the next revision so that all four read alike. Until then, guidance should require the basis of the estimate to be recorded.

15

Three criteria assess an act that leaves no artefact. ZFF-GV-03 3.1 requires a policy check made before proceeding, and the checking is a moment, not a document. ZFF-GV-03 3.2 requires information to be removed or withheld, and an absence cannot be shown. ZFF-AI-03 1.5 requires the learner to describe what happens to information entered into the AI tools their workplace uses, which many learners cannot establish because the vendor does not say and the employer has not written it down.

These are the right things to assess. They are the three where a centre is most likely to accept a bare assertion.

Guidance only Name the evidence route for each. For 3.1 and 3.2, a contemporaneous record made by the learner plus targeted oral questioning in the professional discussion. For ZFF-AI-03 1.5, the tool's own published terms and the workplace policy, with the learner recording where the answer could not be found and what they did about it.

16

ZFF-GV-03 5.3 judges the workplace's rules rather than the learner's own work. It asks the learner to evaluate how effective the workplace's rules were in the situations met. The unit's own boundary note already says to watch this one, and the unit aim says the learner's duty ends at reporting.

On balance it holds, because "in the situations met" ties the judgement to cases the learner personally encountered rather than to the policy as a whole. That is a review of the learner's own experience of a rule, which is at level. It is a fine distinction and it is not written down anywhere.

No change Keep 5.3 as worded, and state the reasoning in the assessment guidance so that a centre does not read it as an invitation to appraise organisational policy.

17

Tested and holding. Three things were looked for across all 145 criteria and not found, and they are worth recording as checked.

No criterion asks an assessor to judge an attitude, a level of confidence or a state of mind. ZFF-GM-03 is the unit most at risk of it and its design note is doing real work. No criterion uses analyse, critically analyse, design or reformulate, and the only appearances of those words are in the level checks that rule them out and in the ZFF-PJ-03 aim at item 4 above. Scope stays personal in every criterion, subject to item 1.

A small cluster of criteria are bare acts and would sit below Level 3 if read alone: ZFF-AI-03 3.2, ZFF-GM-03 3.2 and ZFF-PJ-03 3.1 all simply require the learner to do the thing. Each is the load-bearing step that a later criterion in the same outcome reviews or evaluates, which is how a Level 3 outcome is properly built.

No change Recorded as checked.
