Section 1

What Future Fluency is

Future Fluency is about working well alongside AI and automation. Not building the technology. Using it, checking it, and leading a team through what it changes.

The subject moves as the level rises, and it moves deliberately.

  • At Level 3 the learner changes their own daily work. They use AI tools on real tasks, check what comes back, automate something they repeat, and review what changed.
  • At Level 5 the learner leads a department through the same shift. Technical depth does not rise. Judgement, analysis and working with people does.

This matters for reviewing the specifications. A reader expecting deeper AI content at Level 5 will think the units have gone soft. They have not. The level rises through the complexity of the problem, not the difficulty of the tool.

This page specifies the Level 3 qualifications. The Level 5 Diploma is specified in the same pack and is not reproduced here.

The share of guided learning falls as the level rises, and that is the descriptor doing its work. A Level 5 problem is only broadly defined. A large part of what the learner needs cannot be known in advance by anyone, including the tutor. The department, its systems and its people are the content, and they are found in the doing.

Two things are easy to misread in that table.

Guided learning is not the same as time with Zavmo. It is the hours spent under direction. Tutoring is one part of that, not all of it.

The other hours are not all project work. They are reflection, reading and personal investigation as well as the project, and most of it happens inside the learner's own job.

Section 3

The Level 3 units

The level rule these six are written to

Every criterion is held against the Ofqual RQF Level 3 descriptor: problems that, while well defined, may be complex and non-routine; the learner can interpret and evaluate relevant information and ideas, is aware of different perspectives or approaches, uses appropriate investigation to inform actions, and reviews how effective methods and actions have been.

Deliberately absent throughout: analyse (Level 4), critically analyse and design (Level 6), reformulate (Level 7).

Scope stays personal in every unit. The moment an outcome reaches a colleague, a team or a department, it has left Level 3.

ZFF-AI-03

Using and Checking AI Tools in Daily Work

Level3TQT70 hoursCredits7GLH35 indicativeStatusmandatoryCriteria28

Unit aim

This unit gives the learner the skills to use everyday AI tools on the tasks they already carry out in their own job, and to check what those tools produce before it is used. Learners find out what a large language model does when it answers a question, and why it can be wrong while reading as certain. They then select real tasks from their own working week, write and improve the instructions they give the tool, check the output against other sources, correct it, and review what has changed about the way they work. ZFF-GM-03

Developing a Growth Mindset for Changing Work

Level3TQT50 hoursCredits5GLH25 indicativeStatusmandatoryCriteria22

This unit gives the learner the skills to keep their own working practices up to date as AI and automation change what their job involves. Learners describe what is changing in the tasks they carry out, compare that with the skills they hold, plan and carry out one piece of their own learning, apply the new skill to a real task, seek and act on feedback, and review what it changed.

A design note that is load-bearing: this unit assesses what the learner does, not the attitude they report holding. Every criterion is an observable action or a description of a real event. Level check

This is the unit most at risk of drifting above Level 3, because extended project work invites higher-order language. Three things hold it down.

The brief is given. "Improve one recurring process in your own work" is stated for the learner. They choose the instance, not the problem. The problem therefore remains well defined, and the complexity comes from the process and its failures.

The verbs stay at level. Establish, record, identify, select, state, carry out, interpret, evaluate, review. Not analyse (4), not design or critically evaluate (6), not reformulate (7).

The scope stays personal. 5.4 recommends to the learner's own manager about the learner's own process. 5.3 evaluates against the learner's own stated expectation, not against organisational objectives.

What would push it to Level 4: letting the learner choose which of several processes to improve on comparative benefit, or asking them to evaluate the improvement's implications beyond their own role. The assessment guidance states both exclusions, because a motivated learner will drift into them and an assessor may not notice.

Section 6

Assessment and quality assurance

The same model applies to every unit.

Portfolio of evidence from real work. Assessment is by portfolio drawn from work the learner is already responsible for, gathered over the period of the unit. There is no examination.

The AI teaches. A human assessor decides. Zavmo's AI tutors deliver the learning and prompt the learner to produce and submit evidence. The AI does not make the assessment decision. Every decision on every criterion is made by a named, occupationally competent, appropriately qualified human assessor, internally verified by a qualified IQA against a sampling plan, and sampled by Gateway's External Quality Assurer.

How evidence is captured. Learner activity in the platform is captured as xAPI statements and assembled into the portfolio: the tasks they selected, the instruction sets they wrote and rewrote, the outputs the tool returned, the checks they ran, the amendments they made and the reasons they gave. This gives the assessor a time-stamped record of the learner's process rather than only a finished artefact. That is unusually strong evidence for criteria such as ZFF-AI-03 3.3 and 4.6. The xAPI record is evidence presented to the assessor. It is not an assessment decision.

Professional discussion is mandatory. At least one recorded professional discussion per unit, conducted by the qualified assessor. This is the primary control for authenticating the learner at distance and confirming the portfolio is their own work.

Declared AI use. Learners are expected to use AI in producing evidence, because that is the subject of the qualification. All AI use in evidence must be declared. The assessment decision rests on the learner's judgement about the AI output, not on the polish of the output itself, and that judgement is confirmed in live discussion.

Suggested evidence. Records of professional discussion and oral questioning for the knowledge outcomes. Product evidence, annotated by the learner, for the skills outcomes. Witness testimony from a line manager where a criterion turns on something that happened in real work. Reflective accounts for the review outcomes. These are suggestions and centres could use other evidence, provided the criteria are covered and the evidence can be internally and externally verified.

Real-work grounding. The tasks used must be tasks the learner is already responsible for. Simulated tasks should only be used where a learner's role genuinely offers no suitable real task, and the reason must be recorded.

Section 7

What we know is not yet right

Listed plainly, because we would rather you had this list than found it. These are the items from the full pack that bear on Level 3. One further item in the pack concerns two Level 5 units and is not repeated here.

1

Nine of the eleven units are drafted but not reviewed by an assessor. They are written to the descriptors and internally checked. Nobody with assessment experience has read them. This is the largest single gap.

2

TQT is allocated, not measured. The unit hours are judgements that produce a defensible total. If any unit's hours are revised down, both Diplomas need re-checking against the 370 threshold. The Level 3 Diploma has only 20 hours of headroom.

3

Whether AI-led tutoring counts as guided learning is unresolved. We have shown GLH as indicative throughout. The taught and applied split in section 2.4 depends on the answer and we would like Gateway's view.

4

Whether knowledge criteria can be evidenced through recorded tutoring dialogue rather than written tests is unresolved. We have assumed they can, which is favourable to our model, so it should not be taken on our say-so. If written assessment is required for knowledge outcomes, section 2.4 needs revisiting.

5

The assessor layer does not exist yet. We do not currently employ occupationally competent assessors or a qualified IQA. The model in section 6 describes what we will put in place, not what is running today. We would like to talk about the sequence.

6

The two Diplomas have not been checked against qualifications already on the register. ZFF-DA-03 is the unit most likely to overlap something existing.

7

Demand figures are excluded on purpose. We hold internal estimates of which fluencies are most in demand. They are not evidence and they are not in this document.
