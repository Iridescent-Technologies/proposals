These screens show the pathway builder as we are building it. Some steps are live today and others are in build.

Step 1 of 16

What a pathway is

A pathway is a programme of learning for a group of people. It takes them from where they are to a clear change at work.

You set one up in nine chapters. Each chapter answers one question.

  1. Purpose. Why does it exist?
  2. Learners. Who is it for?
  3. Sources. What do we teach from?
  4. Outcomes. What will they be able to do?
  5. Teaching. How do we teach it and check it?
  6. See and do. Games, pictures and badges.
  7. Preview. What will a learner see?
  8. Launch. Who gets it, and by when?
  9. After. Did it change what they do?

Beside every pathway sits Skills. It records where each learner started and where they finished.

Step 2 of 16

How it adapts to each learner

Most training gives everyone the same course. A Zavmo pathway is designed to adapt to each person. That is how a learner builds skill, not only knowledge.

It starts where they are

At Discover, a short check of their skills places each learner. They skip what they can already do.

It uses their own job

At Discuss, they tell us about their role. Zavmo teaches at their level and takes every example from their own work. The learning is about their job, not a case study about somebody else's.

Practice turns knowing into doing

After each module they do one real task at work. Games give them practice inside each lesson. Where their confidence is low, they get more practice.

It checks they can do it

Demonstrate asks for evidence that they can do the work, not only know about it. Skills then records the change from where they started to where they finished. It scores practice (knows it, has applied it, uses it routinely) and confidence.

Compliance is the exception

Some training must be the same for everyone, such as fire safety. Switch a pathway to compliance and every learner does everything. Nothing is skipped and nothing adapts.

Step 3 of 16

Build it by talking to Zavmo

You do not have to fill nine chapters by hand. You talk to Zavmo, by voice or by typing.

Zavmo asks what it needs. As you answer, it fills each chapter and shows you what it wrote.

Every chapter opens already filled with our recommendation. You check it, and change anything you disagree with.

The conversation is the fast way in. The chapters are where you check the detail.

Step 4 of 16 · Chapter 1

Purpose

Purpose says why the pathway exists. It asks two plain questions.

  • What should people do differently at work?
  • What will show it worked?

A pathway that names a change at work can be measured. One that does not ends as a course people finished.

Purpose also sets the compliance switch. On means every learner does everything. Nothing is skipped, nothing adapts, lessons go in order and Demonstrate is required. Off means Zavmo adapts to each learner.

Step 5 of 16 · Chapter 2

Learners

Learners says who the pathway is for.

The same content lands differently for a new starter and for an expert. Knowing who they are sets the pace.

  • Who gets it: by job role, department or location.
  • Their country and language.
  • What they must know before they start.

Learners choose their own time, in learn. You can set a date to finish by, in Discuss.

Each learner tells Zavmo their own needs, once. Captions, screen reader support and readable text are on for everyone.

Step 6 of 16 · Chapter 3

Sources

Sources is what Zavmo teaches from. Zavmo teaches only what it can point to.

That can be a recognised standard, a Future Fluency unit, or your own organisation's material. Learners trust a pathway built on something they recognise.

Recognised qualifications are the benchmark. Describe the pathway, and Zavmo matches it to over 1,000 recognised qualifications from over 80 awarding bodies. Awarding bodies are the organisations that set and certify qualifications.

You do not need your own content. Zavmo already holds the learning specifications to build from: recognised qualifications and its own Future Fluency units.

If you have your own material, drag and drop it straight in: policies, slides, handbooks and specifications. Zavmo teaches from it alongside the standard.

Each source carries its level, hours and credit.

Step 7 of 16 · Chapter 4

Outcomes

Outcomes say what learners will be able to do. They are set for the whole pathway and for each module.

Each outcome has assessment criteria: the things a learner must show. Every lesson and every piece of evidence traces back to one.

Each outcome and criterion also has a Bloom level. Bloom's taxonomy is a scale of how deep a task goes. It runs from remembering a fact to creating something new. It sets the level each learner must show.

Zavmo sets every level. You change one only if you disagree. If a criterion is never assessed, Zavmo tells you.

Step 8 of 16 · Teaching, stage 1 of 4

Discover

Teaching has four stages: Discover, Discuss, Deliver and Demonstrate. This is where the pathway meets each learner.

Discover finds out what a learner can already do, so nobody sits through it again.

It starts with a needs assessment, often called a TNA (training needs assessment). It is a short check of their skills.

A module they already pass is skipped. A learner who rates themselves highly answers two quick questions first.

Questions every learner answers once, such as their job title, sit in Settings. Discover asks only what this pathway needs.

Step 9 of 16 · Teaching, stage 2 of 4

Discuss

Discuss is about the learner's own role.

They answer a few questions about their job. Their answers shape the examples in every lesson that follows. So the learning is about their work, not a case study.

They choose their own time and book their own sessions. You decide whether there is a date to finish by. Their manager gets one page on what to expect and how to help.

Step 10 of 16 · Teaching, stage 3 of 4

Deliver

Deliver teaches each module at the learner's level, then asks them to use it at work.

Lessons begin where the learner is, not at the first page.

After each module they do one real task on the job. In this example, that is drafting a rota with AI.

Where their confidence is low, they get more practice. Zavmo comes back to anything they got wrong. Each session opens with what they did last time.

Step 11 of 16 · Teaching, stage 4 of 4

Demonstrate

Demonstrate checks that a learner can do it, not only that they know it.

They choose how to show their evidence: a report, a proposal, a presentation or a recorded explanation. Every criterion is assessed at least once.

They get feedback on each criterion, and can send their work again. They confirm it is their own work, and say how they used AI.

Then someone signs it off. That can be their manager, someone in your learning team, or an assessor you name.

Sign-off is the only route to a certificate. No sign-off, no certificate.

Step 12 of 16 · Chapter 6

See and do

See and do holds the games, pictures and badges. Practice and pictures make learning stick.

Games give practice inside every module. Zavmo picks the games that suit each module, and you can see every one.

Pictures and diagrams are made in your own colours. They explain objectives, outcomes and the steps in a process.

Badges mark a learner's progress. You see every badge the pathway gives, and approve each one before launch. A badge you do not approve is not given.

You can switch off anything that gets in your people's way.

Step 13 of 16 · Chapter 7

Preview

Before anyone else sees it, you walk the pathway as a learner.

You pick the kind of learner, such as a team lead new to AI. You see what they would see, from the first question to the certificate.

Nothing you do counts as learning or shows in reports. Tell Zavmo anything that confused you, and it changes it.

Step 14 of 16 · Chapter 8

Launch

Launch sends the pathway out to the right people.

  • You publish a version, and give it to people, a group or a department.
  • You set whether it is required, and when it is due.
  • Managers are told, so they know to support their people.
  • You choose the certificate, and a cover picture or video.
  • Zavmo alerts you if anything needs a look.

Step 15 of 16 · Chapter 9

After

The pathway does not stop at the certificate. People forget most of what they do not use, so we follow up at work.

  • At the end, each learner answers one question: what did you think?
  • At the end, their manager is asked what has changed at work.
  • A month later, Zavmo sends a reminder and a quick check. Have you used it? They rate their skills again.

Their answers sit in reports, beside where they started and finished.

Step 16 of 16

Skills: where each learner started and finished

Skills is one record of what each person can do. It builds up across every pathway they take.

It measures the same skills three times.

  1. StartDiscoverThe skills check places the learner, and passed modules are skipped.
  2. FinishDemonstrateThe same skills are checked again, for example by their manager.
  3. A month laterAfterThey rate the skills again, once they have used them at work.
PracticeKnows it, has applied it, uses it routinely.
ConfidenceHow sure they are.

High confidence with little practice is where a learner needs support most.

The next pathway they take starts where this one ended.
