The problem

The gap you can't currently see: certified vs capable.

Your people hold the certificates. Compliance is green. Completion rates look healthy. And none of it tells you the one thing that matters: can they actually do the work the next two years will ask of them?

A completion is an attendance record. A quiz score is a memory test. A self-assessment is a feeling. None of them is evidence of capability. And all of them go stale the moment the role changes.

And roles are changing faster than the frameworks built to describe them.

The distance between the two is invisible to almost every learning system. They measure the wrong thing.

2030
The UK's national occupational classification was finalised before ChatGPT existed, on a ~10-year cycle. Its next update isn't expected until around 2030. By design, a snapshot of a world that's already moved on.
safety ≫ uncertainty
In our analysis of the UK's national occupational standards, safety and correct procedure are addressed dozens of times more often than working under uncertainty. They are near-silent on the skill your people most need.
The framework · our IP

Three layers, one honest loop.

Most "future skills" frameworks are a list. Future Fluency is a system, built on one idea: every question a growing person asks is really about a capability. Something you can actually do. Three layers sit over that idea.

You are not certified in a Fluency. You are more or less fluent in it. Exactly like a language.

Layer 3: Degree of fluencyWhere do you stand?

For each Fluency, a person is somewhere on a path. It runs from foundational (you have it) to functional (your role uses it now) to future (it's coming for you). The same skill is "future" for one person and "foundational" for another.

Personal by design. A relation between a Fluency and a person, never a folder.

The same Fluency, three degrees, one moment. Take AI Fluency: it's future for a finance director, functional for a data analyst, and foundational for a new graduate. There is no "future skills" folder. "Is this a future skill?" has no answer until you name whose future.

The moat · the piece they can't do

Grounded in the UK's approved standards.

Anyone can generate a course. What no learning tool can do is guarantee that a real job maps to the right skills, and those skills to regulated, assessed learning. Future Fluency is pinned to the government-approved systems that define UK skills. So a person's path is real, auditable, and theirs to carry. It teaches only where the job, the standard and the qualification meet.

The job

What this role really requires

The job description anchors learning to the actual role, not a generic template. It is the "for this person, in this job" filter that makes personalisation legitimate.

The standard · NOS

What competent practice looks like

National Occupational Standards define, nationally, the skills and behaviours a competent person demonstrates. An external, published definition of "good". Not the model's opinion.

The qualification · OFQUAL

Regulated, assessed learning

OFQUAL-regulated qualifications supply accredited content and assessment criteria. A regulator already stands behind that curriculum. It leads to a recognised credential.

This is the piece competitors can't do. Zavmo maps the role to its NOS skills, pulls the matching OFQUAL content from the standards graph, and teaches that. If a thing can't be traced to a framework, it isn't taught. So "why were they taught this?" always has an answer your compliance team can stand behind.

The proof

Evidence, not self-report.

Here's the test any L&D leader should hold a "future skills" vendor to: when your people learn something, can you prove they can now do it? Or only that they turned up?

The classic model of training evaluation (Kirkpatrick) describes four levels. Most platforms stop at the first two: reactions and recall. Completion rates and quiz scores live there: the easiest things to measure, and the least worth knowing.

Future Fluency is built to operate where it actually matters. At capability.
